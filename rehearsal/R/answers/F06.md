# F06 - FOMC decision, 8-9 December 2026

Source: evidence pack /home/claude/cp/R/pack.md only (21 official documents, cutoff 2026-11-23T07:06Z). Inferences are marked as such.

## 1. What the Committee did and what it signals

Facts from the pack
- September 15-16: raised the target range 25 bp to 3-3/4 to 4 percent ("removed a dose of accommodation", Warsh). Upper bound before the December meeting is 4.00.
- October 27-28: held, 10-2. Cook and Hammack dissented in favor of a 25 bp hike. Statement: "The accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation... The Committee will deliver price stability."
- September SEP dots for end-2026 (midpoint now 3.875): 2 at 3.875 (no more hikes), 12 at 4.125 (one more), 4 at 4.375 (two more). With October a hold, December is the only meeting left, so 16 of 18 participants had pencilled in at least one more hike by year-end.
- October minutes: "almost all participants judged that... further action to raise the target range - broadly consistent with the September SEP - would likely be appropriate" in the months ahead. "Some" wanted to hike in October; "a few" holders called it a close call; "some" said market pricing of tightening "in December and next year" matched their own view of the appropriate path; "a couple" said more than 25 bp "could warrant consideration at the next meeting if incoming inflation data were unfavorable".
- Market pricing as reported in the minutes: "high probabilities assigned to a 25 basis point hike by year-end"; Desk survey median expects one hike in December and another in March 2027.
- Speakers since the meeting:
  - Waller (Nov 10): "I would still support a 25 basis point increase... at our December meeting" even if October CPI improved; if core CPI came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher... a 50 basis point hike... should be considered". Also: what matters is that priced-in hikes "are eventually carried out"; if not, rates fall and policy loosens "in an undesirable way".
  - Cook (Nov 20): dissenter, "waiting to act... has a cost", October data "fail to provide me with sufficient evidence that inflation is returning to a sustainable path".
  - Barr (Nov 18): "I expect it will be appropriate to raise rates further" if his forecast holds.
  - Jefferson (Nov 13): non-committal on timing, but "I see the trajectory of inflation as providing the more important signal".
  - Bowman (Nov 10): most cautious. "The benefit of additional policy tightening has to be carefully weighed against its costs for interest-rate-sensitive sectors"; wants clarity on market volatility and AI financing; yet "there is still work to do to bring inflation closer to 2 percent".
  - Warsh (Oct 28 presser, Nov 16 remarks): refuses forward guidance and will not prejudge December; calls the interval "information rich"; "we don't just talk - we act"; tighter financial conditions tell him "policy is working".

Inference: the centre of the Committee skipped October on purpose (gradualism, with the December hike already priced) and intends to deliver in December unless something breaks. No one in the pack argues for easing.

## 2. Latest official data and which way each pushes

Inflation - pushes toward a hike
- October CPI: headline +0.4 percent m/m, core +0.3 percent for the third straight month; shelter +0.3, transportation services +0.8, food +0.5, recreation +0.8. Broad-based. This meets Waller's stated threshold for "considering" 50 bp.
- September PCE: +0.5 m/m, 3.6 percent y/y; core +0.3 m/m, 3.1 percent y/y. Officials estimate October PCE at about 3.8 percent headline and 3.2-3.3 percent core (Cook, Bowman) - moving up, not down.
- Beige Book (through Oct 5): price growth robust in 3 districts, moderate in 8, pace picking up in 4; oil costs "permeated all District reports".
- Mild offsets: energy CPI slowed to +0.7 from +5.2; Brent fell "sharply" in early November on hopes of a US-Iran agreement; tariff effects waning; Q3 quarterly core PCE 2.5 percent annualised; longer-run expectations described as anchored.

Employment - permits a hike, does not demand one
- October payrolls +165,000, prior two months revised up 84,000; unemployment 4.1 percent; average hourly earnings +0.4 percent m/m, 3.1 percent y/y; claims near historical lows.
- Soft spots: involuntary part-time +339,000; long-term unemployed 27.6 percent of the unemployed; "low hire, low fire". Not enough to argue for easing.

Activity - pushes toward a hike, with a financial-conditions caveat
- Q3 GDP +3.4 percent; final sales to private domestic purchasers +4.9 percent; AI-led investment boom broadening.
- Caveats: real PCE flat in September, real DPI -0.3 percent, saving rate 3.8 percent; housing and other rate-sensitive sectors weakening; Beige Book describes mostly slight-to-modest growth.
- Financial conditions have tightened since September: higher mortgage rates, wider high-yield and AI-related spreads, falling AI equity valuations "these past couple of weeks" (Barr, Nov 16), French sovereign stress, stronger dollar. The October FOMC opened with a financial-stability review focused on AI-related leverage. This is the main factor pulling toward a hold.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

- Cut of 50 bp or more: only a disorderly AI-debt or hedge-fund deleveraging crash that impairs market functioning before December 9, severe enough to override 3.8 percent inflation. Even then the Committee's own discussion pointed to liquidity tools and the discount window, not the policy rate. Near-zero.
- Cut of 25 bp: the same shock in milder form, plus a sudden Middle East settlement collapsing oil and a very weak November jobs report. Nothing in the pack points this way; every speaker has inflation as the focus. Tail only.
- Hold: the Committee already held once in a strong economy to "accumulate more information"; the Chair rejects forward guidance and feels no obligation to validate market pricing; Bowman is openly weighing the costs of tightening; AI equities and credit have been selling off for two weeks; oil has fallen; financial conditions are doing some of the work ("policy is working"); political pressure from the White House against the Board is explicit. If market stress deepens into December, "wait for January" is an easy consensus.
- Hike of 25 bp: 16 of 18 dots required it; almost all participants called further tightening likely appropriate; some explicitly tied their October patience to December being priced; Waller says he would support it even with better data and warns that not delivering would ease conditions; data since October (payrolls, core CPI, PCE estimates) all went the hawks' way; two dissenters already there; the midterms are past; the statement language ("will deliver price stability") and the Chair's "we act" framing would be hard to square with a second pass.
- Hike of 50 bp or more: Waller's stated trigger (core CPI at 0.3 or higher) was met and he said a 25 bp move alone "would put our credibility at risk"; a couple of participants already floated more than 25 bp for December; four dots implied two more hikes this year, which after the October skip can only be reached with 50 bp; staff flag de-anchoring risk after more than five years of above-target inflation.

## 4. What I am least sure of

- Depth of the AI-related market sell-off between now and December 9 (inference: the pack shows it is under way but gives no magnitude). This is the main reason hold is not in single digits, and it also caps the 50 bp probability - a Committee worried about hedge-fund leverage and opaque AI debt is unlikely to double the step size.
- How the Chair weighs the decision. He does not submit a dot, disclaims guidance, and presided over a hold that surprised some. I read his rhetoric as hawkish on inflation, but his preference for timing is opaque. This widens the hold/hike25 split.
- How many members follow Waller toward 50. Only "a couple" raised it in October, the centre preferred gradualism, and Bowman, Jefferson and Barr give no hint of a larger move. I treat 50 bp as a real but minority outcome.
- Data still to come before the meeting: October PCE (Nov 25), Q3 GDP second estimate, November jobs (Dec 4), and oil/Iran developments. November CPI arrives Dec 10, after the decision. A weak jobs print plus lower oil would shift mass from hike25 to hold; a hot PCE print would shift a little toward hike50.
- Governor Cook's status (a White House hearing on her future was mentioned). Losing one hawkish vote would not change the majority.

Net effect: the direction is not in doubt (hold or hike, about 98.5 percent combined); the uncertainty is concentrated in whether financial-market stress or the Chair's patience produces a second skip.
