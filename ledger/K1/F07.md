# F07 forecast: FOMC decision, 8-9 December 2026

Evidence: cp/K1/pack.md only (22 official documents, captured 2026-11-26). The upper bound in effect before the meeting is 4.00 percent (target range 3-3/4 to 4 percent).

## 1. What the Committee did and what it signals

**Recent actions**
- September 15-16: raised the range 25 bp to 3-3/4 to 4 percent ("removed a dose of accommodation").
- October 27-28: held, by a 10-2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike.
- The October statement said "the accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance" and "The Committee will deliver price stability."

**September SEP dots for end-2026**
- 12 of 18 participants at 4.125 (one more hike), 4 at 4.375 (two more), 2 at 3.875 (none).
- December is the last meeting of 2026, so 16 of 18 had pencilled in at least one more hike by this meeting.

**October minutes**
- "Almost all participants" judged that further action to raise the range, "broadly consistent with the September Summary of Economic Projections", would likely be appropriate.
- "Some" participants wanted to hike in October; "a few" who voted to hold called it a close call.
- Some said market pricing of tightening "in December and next year" matched their own view of the appropriate path, and several saw that as what made holding safe.
- "A couple" said more than 25 bp could warrant consideration in December if inflation data were unfavourable.
- Futures showed "high probabilities assigned to a 25 basis point hike by year-end"; the Desk survey median expected a December hike.

**Speakers since the meeting**
- Waller (Nov 10): he would support 25 bp in December even if inflation improved. If October core CPI came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher", a 50 bp hike "should be considered", and "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk". He also said that not carrying out signalled hikes would loosen policy in an undesirable way.
- Cook (Nov 20): "waiting to act ... has a cost"; she sees no evidence of disinflation since September. She did not name a size.
- Barr (Nov 18): "I expect it will be appropriate to raise rates further."
- Jefferson (Nov 13): inflation's trajectory provides "the more important signal"; no explicit commitment.
- Bowman (Nov 10): the most cautious. "There is still work to do", but the benefit of more tightening "has to be carefully weighed against its costs", and she wants clarity on market volatility and AI financing.
- Warsh (Oct 28 press conference): refuses forward guidance and will not prejudge the meeting, but said "we don't just talk—we act" and called the coming period "information rich". His Nov 16 remarks carried no policy signal.

No speaker in the pack argues for a further pause, and none mentions easing.

## 2. Latest official data

**Inflation pushes toward a hike, and toward some debate about size.**
- October CPI: headline +0.4 percent, core +0.3 percent for the third straight month. This meets Waller's stated condition for considering 50 bp.
- October PCE prices: +0.3 percent headline and core on the month; 3.8 percent headline and 3.3 percent core over 12 months. Core was 3.1 percent in September, so it is moving away from target.
- Beige Book: prices rose moderately, and contacts expect further increases.
- Offsets: energy rose only 0.7 percent in October after 5.2 percent in September. Bowman reports Brent fell sharply in early November on hopes of a U.S.-Iran agreement.

**Employment pushes toward a hike, or at least removes any reason to wait.**
- October payrolls +165,000, with August and September revised up by 84,000 combined.
- Unemployment 4.1 percent; average hourly earnings +0.4 percent on the month and 3.1 percent over 12 months.
- Softer details: low hiring, long-term unemployed at 27.6 percent of the unemployed, and involuntary part-time work up 339,000.

**Activity is strong in the rear view and softer at the margin.**
- Q3 real GDP was revised up to 4.3 percent; real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 5.8 percent.
- Real PCE was flat in October, and the saving rate was 4.1 percent.
- Beige Book: eight Districts reported slight to moderate growth and four no change; consumer spending was little changed; housing and construction weakened.

**Financial conditions are the main argument for caution.**
- AI and tech equities fell and credit spreads widened in late October and early November (Bowman, Barr, the minutes).
- Mortgage rates are higher and the dollar is stronger.
- Warsh describes this tightening as policy working, not as a reason to stop.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

**Cut of 50 bp or more.** This needs a disorderly AI-equity and credit collapse with impaired market functioning in the next two weeks, along the lines of the tail scenarios staff presented in October. Even then, liquidity tools would come first. With core PCE at 3.3 percent and rising, a large cut is nearly unthinkable.

**Cut of 25 bp.** Same shock, smaller response, or an abrupt collapse in the December 4 jobs report. No participant has hinted at easing, and all but two of the September dots had rates higher at year-end.

**Hold.**
- Warsh will not pre-commit, and the Committee already surprised hawks once by holding in October.
- Bowman's cost-benefit framing and the AI repricing give cover to wait.
- Real consumption stalled in October, oil has eased on peace hopes, and housing is weakening.
- The November CPI arrives on December 10, the day after the decision.
- A sharp further equity or credit selloff, or a weak November payroll report, could tip a majority to wait until January.

**Hike of 25 bp.**
- It is the path the SEP, the minutes and market pricing all point to.
- Every data release since October has been at least as strong as expected on inflation, jobs and GDP.
- Two members already voted for it, and Waller and Barr have effectively said they will join them; several October holders called it a close call.
- Many participants were anxious that the October hold not be read as weaker resolve.
- Failing to deliver would ease conditions against the Committee's wishes, which is Waller's argument.

**Hike of 50 bp or more.**
- Waller's own trigger was met exactly, and he tied 25 bp to a credibility risk.
- A couple of participants raised more than 25 bp in October.
- Core PCE rose to 3.3 percent, GDP was revised to 4.3 percent, and payrolls were strong.
- Cook warned that delay means "larger rate increases" later.
- A Chairman who wants to show "no soft inflation target" could choose a decisive move.
- Against: ten members held in October, a couple favoured gradualism, markets are fragile, and markets are priced for 25.

## 4. What I am least sure of

- **How Warsh steers.** The pack shows a Chairman who dislikes guidance and held in October despite a hawkish-leaning Committee. I cannot tell how much of that hold was tactical. This keeps hold in the low-to-mid teens rather than single digits.
- **Financial markets between now and December 9.** The pack documents an AI-related selloff "these past couple of weeks" as of November 16, but not its size or whether it continued. A disorderly turn is the main route to hold and the only route to a cut. This widens the left side slightly.
- **How many would back 50.** Waller's conditional is explicit, but he said "considered", and I see only "a couple" of such voices. I judge 50 bp dissents more likely than a 50 bp outcome.
- **Data still to come.** The November employment report (December 4) lands before the meeting; a large surprise either way could shift roughly 5 points between hold and hike. I have no market pricing later than the October minutes.
- **Small inconsistencies in the pack.** Speakers give slightly different 12-month PCE figures (Cook 3.2 percent core for October against BEA's 3.3). I use BEA's.

Net: the signalling and the data are unusually aligned toward a 25 bp hike. Uncertainty about the Chairman and about markets stops me going above the high 70s, with the remainder split between hold and a larger hike.
