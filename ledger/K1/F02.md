# F02 forecast: FOMC decision, 8-9 December 2026

Source: cp/K1/pack.md only (22 official documents, captured 2026-11-26). The upper bound in effect before the meeting is 4.00 percent (target range 3-3/4 to 4 percent).

## 1. What the Committee did and what it is signalling

**Recent actions**
- September 15-16: raised the range 25 bp to 3-3/4 to 4 percent ("removed a dose of accommodation", in Chairman Warsh's words).
- October 27-28: held, by a 10-2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike. The statement said "the accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance" and "The Committee will deliver price stability."

**September SEP (Doc 5)**
- End-2026 funds rate dots: 12 at 4.125, 4 at 4.375, 2 at 3.875. The median of 4.1 is one more 25 bp hike from the current range.
- With October a hold, December is the only 2026 meeting left. So 16 of 18 participants in September wrote down a path that now requires at least a 25 bp hike in December, and 4 wrote down one that requires 50 bp.
- 17 of 18 saw inflation risks weighted to the upside.

**October minutes (Doc 1)**
- "Almost all participants" judged that further increases "broadly consistent with the September SEP would likely be appropriate."
- "Some" said market-implied tightening "in December and next year" matched their view of the appropriate path, and several saw that alignment as reducing the risk of holding in October.
- "Some" wanted to hike in October. A few holders called October "a close call."
- "A couple" said more than 25 bp could warrant consideration at the next meeting if inflation data were unfavourable.
- Counterweights: a majority noted financial conditions had tightened; a couple favoured a "gradual approach to policy firming"; many wanted to assess the durability of AI investment demand and financing.
- Markets at the time: futures gave a "high probability" to a 25 bp hike by year-end; the Desk survey median expected a hike in December and another in March 2027.

**Speakers since the meeting**
- Waller (Nov 10): "I would still support a 25 basis point increase ... at our December meeting" even if October inflation improved. If core CPI came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher," then "a 50 basis point hike ... should be considered." He also said promised hikes must be carried out "in a reasonable period of time" or markets would unwind them, loosening policy.
- Cook (Nov 20): repeated her case for hiking; "waiting to act ... has a cost"; data since September "fail to provide me with sufficient evidence that inflation is returning to a sustainable path." She did not mention 50 bp.
- Barr (Nov 18): "I expect it will be appropriate to raise rates further."
- Jefferson (Nov 13): "I see the trajectory of inflation as providing the more important signal." He did not commit to a date.
- Bowman (Nov 10): the most cautious. "The benefit of additional policy tightening has to be carefully weighed against its costs for interest-rate-sensitive sectors"; she wants clarity on AI financing and market volatility. But also "inflation pressures are persisting, while labor market conditions appear to be firming ... still work to do."
- Warsh (Oct 28 press conference, Nov 16 remarks): rejects forward guidance and will not prejudge. He called the coming interval "information rich," said the Committee has "shown our willingness to act," and that tighter financial conditions tell him "policy is working." His November 16 remarks had no policy content.

**Net read:** the Committee has a tightening bias, the median path implies a December hike, and at least four voters (Cook, Hammack, Waller, Barr) are on record leaning to a hike. The Chair has kept his options open.

## 2. Latest data

**Inflation: pushes toward a hike, and gives some cover for 50**
- October CPI (Doc 20): headline +0.4 percent; core +0.3 percent for the third month running. That is exactly the threshold Waller named for considering 50 bp. Transportation services +0.8, recreation +0.8, food +0.5.
- October PCE (Doc 21): headline +0.3 percent m/m and 3.8 percent y/y (3.6 to 3.7 in September); core +0.3 percent m/m and 3.3 percent y/y (3.1 in September). Core is moving away from target, and both are near the top of the SEP's 2026 ranges.
- Beige Book: prices up moderately, St. Louis and Cleveland "robust," and contacts expect further increases.
- One softer number: the Q3 PCE price index in the GDP release is 2.1 percent annualised (core 2.5), well below the 12-month rates. I give it little weight because it sits oddly with the monthly prints.

**Employment: no obstacle to a hike**
- October payrolls +165,000, with +84,000 in revisions to August and September. Unemployment 4.1 percent. Average hourly earnings +0.4 percent m/m, 3.1 percent y/y.
- Claims are near historical lows (four-week average about 195,000, per Cook).
- Soft spots: part-time for economic reasons +339,000; long-term unemployed 27.6 percent of the total; a "low hire, low fire" market.

**Activity: strong in Q3, softer in Q4**
- Q3 GDP revised up to 4.3 percent from 3.4; real final sales to private domestic purchasers 5.8 percent; GDI 4.4 percent. This is well above the SEP longer-run growth rate of 2.0.
- October real PCE was flat, with the saving rate up to 4.1 percent.
- Beige Book: only "slight to moderate" growth in eight Districts and no change in four; housing, construction and autos weakening on higher rates.

**Financial conditions and energy: the main argument for restraint**
- AI equities fell and credit spreads widened in late October and early November (Bowman, Barr, the minutes).
- Mortgage rates rose sharply and bank equities fell.
- Brent dropped "sharply" in early November on hopes of a US-Iran agreement, after trading above $110.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

**Cut of 50 bp or more.** Only a disorderly financial event in the next two weeks gets here: the AI-debt and hedge-fund-leverage tail scenarios in the Financial Stability Report materialising, with fire sales and impaired Treasury market functioning. Even then the Fed would more likely use liquidity tools first, with inflation at 3.8 percent. Near zero.

**Cut of 25 bp.** The same tail in milder form, plus a very weak November jobs report on December 4. It would require reversing a hiking stance within one meeting while core inflation is rising. Near zero.

**Hold.**
- The Committee already passed once in October with the same broad data picture, preferring to wait.
- The Chair refuses to pre-commit and stresses that tighter financial conditions are doing the work.
- AI equity and credit repricing accelerated in November. Bowman explicitly weighs the costs of more tightening, and many participants wanted to see whether AI investment demand holds up.
- Oil fell sharply on peace hopes; a US-Iran deal before the meeting would cut headline inflation prospects.
- Real spending stalled in October and the Beige Book is soft.
- The November jobs report could disappoint.
- Political pressure on the Board is visible in the pack (the President's remarks, the Cook proceedings).
- A hold with a hawkish statement is the realistic alternative to a hike.

**Hike of 25 bp.**
- It is the SEP median path, and "almost all" participants endorsed further hikes "broadly consistent" with it.
- Markets and the Desk survey priced it, and several participants explicitly leaned on that pricing to justify October's hold. Failing to deliver would ease conditions, as Waller argued.
- Every major release since October has gone the hawkish way: core PCE up to 3.3 percent, three straight 0.3 core CPI prints, payrolls +165,000 with upward revisions, GDP 4.3 percent.
- Two dissenters, plus Waller and Barr on record, plus Jefferson pointing to inflation. Holding again risks a larger dissent bloc and a credibility cost for a Chair who has staked his tenure on "no ifs, ands, or buts."
- The "gradual approach" language and market fragility favour 25 over 50.

**Hike of 50 bp or more.**
- Waller's stated trigger was met: core CPI 0.3. He said that hiking only 25 in that case "would put our credibility at risk."
- "A couple" of participants raised more than 25 at the October meeting.
- Four participants had 4.375 dots for end-2026, which now requires 50 bp in December.
- The Committee skipped October, so 50 would be catching up to its own path. Growth of 4.3 percent and core PCE rising to 3.3 percent would support it.
- Against: no one but Waller has raised it publicly, Cook did not, financial conditions are already tightening with AI credit stress, and Warsh describes policy as working.

## 4. What I am least sure of

1. **The Chair's reaction function.** Warsh gives no guidance, and October showed he will hold even when most colleagues lean to hiking. This is the main reason hold is well above a token probability, despite an SEP path and speaker lineup that would otherwise put a hike near 90 percent.
2. **Market and geopolitical developments I cannot see.** The pack has no market pricing after late October, and the AI selloff and oil drop are described only in passing. A deeper equity or credit slide, or an Iran deal, would move weight from the hikes to hold. This widens the hold tail; it does little for cuts.
3. **The November employment report (December 4).** It arrives before the meeting. A strong print firms up a hike; a sharp negative supports a hold.
4. **How much support 50 bp has.** Waller's condition was literally met, but his later November remarks in the pack (payments, Fed Challenge) are silent on policy, and no other speaker echoes him. I treat 50 as a live minority outcome, not the base case.
5. **Data oddities.** The Q3 PCE price index of 2.1 percent sits awkwardly beside the 12-month figures. If the Committee read quarterly inflation as cooler than the 12-month rates suggest, that would slightly favour 25 over 50, or hold.

Net effect: I am confident about the direction (up or flat, not down), but I keep a meaningful hold probability and a modest 50 bp tail around a 25 bp base case.
