# F05 forecast: FOMC decision, 8-9 December 2026

Source: evidence pack cp/K1/pack.md only (22 official documents, captured 2026-11-26). The upper bound of the target range going into the meeting is 4.00 percent (range 3-3/4 to 4 percent).

## 1. What the Committee did recently and what it signals

**Recent actions**
- September 15-16: raised the range 25 bp to 3-3/4 to 4 percent ("removed a dose of accommodation", in Chairman Warsh's words).
- October 27-28: held, by a 10-2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike.
- The October statement said "the accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance" and ended "The Committee will deliver price stability."

**September SEP (Doc 5)**
- The median end-2026 funds rate is 4.1 percent, one more 25 bp step above the current midpoint.
- Of 18 participants, 12 were at 4.125, 4 at 4.375 and 2 at 3.875. So 16 of 18 pencilled in at least one more hike by year-end.
- December is the only meeting left in 2026.

**October minutes (Doc 1)**
- "Almost all participants" judged that further action to raise the range, "broadly consistent with the September Summary of Economic Projections", would likely be appropriate.
- "Some participants" said market pricing of a December hike and more in 2027 matched their own view of the appropriate path, and several saw that alignment as reducing the risk of holding in October.
- "Some" favoured hiking in October, and "a few" who supported the hold called it a close call.
- "A couple" said a move of more than 25 bp could warrant consideration in December if inflation data were unfavourable.
- "A couple" favoured a gradual approach to firming.
- Market pricing at the time: futures gave a high probability to a 25 bp hike by year-end, and the Desk survey median expected a hike in December and another in March 2027.

**Chairman Warsh (Doc 4, Doc 12)**
- He refuses forward guidance and will not prejudge meetings.
- He described the hold as waiting through an "information-rich" interval to determine the "appropriate protocol".
- He stressed "we don't just talk—we act" and "no ifs, ands, or buts" on price stability.
- He also said financial conditions were tightening and that this showed "policy is working".
- His November 16 remarks were about super intelligence, with no policy signal.

**Speakers since the meeting**
- **Waller (Nov 10, before the CPI release):** tightening is needed "sooner rather than later".
  - He said he would support 25 bp in December even if October inflation improved.
  - He said that if core CPI came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher", a 50 bp hike "should be considered" and that "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk".
  - He argued that the timing of promised hikes matters less than carrying them out within a reasonable time, since otherwise market rates would fall and loosen policy.
- **Cook (Nov 20):** inflation data since September "fail to provide me with sufficient evidence that inflation is returning to a sustainable path"; "waiting to act … has a cost".
- **Barr (Nov 18):** "I expect it will be appropriate to raise rates further" if the economy evolves as he forecasts.
- **Jefferson (Nov 13, Nov 19):** "the trajectory of inflation [is] providing the more important signal"; noncommittal on timing.
- **Bowman (Nov 10):** the most cautious voice.
  - "The benefit of additional policy tightening has to be carefully weighed against its costs for interest-rate-sensitive sectors."
  - She wants clarity on market volatility and AI financing.
  - She also said "there is still work to do", that inflation pressures are persisting and that the labour market is firming.

No official in the pack argues for holding in December, and none mentions easing. The communication points to a 25 bp hike, with one governor openly raising 50.

## 2. What the latest data show

**Inflation: pushes toward a hike, with some push toward a larger one**
- October CPI (Doc 20): headline up 0.4 percent, core up 0.3 percent for the third straight month.
  - That meets the threshold Waller named for considering 50 bp.
  - Increases were broad: recreation, medical care, motor insurance, transportation services up 0.8, food away from home up 0.5.
- October PCE (Doc 21): headline up 0.3 percent on the month and 3.8 percent on the year; core up 0.3 percent on the month and 3.3 percent on the year.
  - Twelve-month headline is up from 3.7 percent in September (Waller's figure; the staff had estimated 3.6), and core is up from 3.1.
  - Twelve-month core is at or drifting above the SEP's 2026 core projection of 3.4 percent.
- Beige Book (Doc 18): prices up moderately, with contacts expecting costs and prices to keep rising. Wage growth ticked up from modest to moderate.
- Mitigating points:
  - Monthly headline inflation slowed from September's 0.5-0.6 percent.
  - Brent fell sharply in early November on hopes of a US-Iran agreement.
  - Average hourly earnings are up only 3.1 percent on the year.
  - Longer-run inflation expectations are described as anchored.

**Employment: removes any labour-market reason to wait**
- October payrolls rose 165,000, with 84,000 of upward revisions to August and September.
- Unemployment is 4.1 percent, at the low end of its range since March.
- Initial claims are near historical lows.
- Soft spots are part-time work for economic reasons (up 339,000), the long-term unemployed share (27.6 percent) and the "low hire, low fire" pattern.
- The Committee sees the labour market at or near maximum employment with balanced risks.

**Activity: strong in the rear-view mirror, softer at the margin**
- Third-quarter GDP was revised up to 4.3 percent from 3.4.
- Real final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 5.8 percent, and GDI rose 4.4 percent.
- Softer current readings:
  - Real PCE was flat in October.
  - The Beige Book reports consumer spending little changed, four Districts with no growth, and weakening housing, autos and construction.
  - AI-related equities fell and credit spreads widened in late October and November.
- The strong data argue for tightening. The softer readings and market stress argue for 25 bp over 50 bp, and are the only real case for a hold.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

**Cut of 50 bp or more**
- This needs an acute financial or economic emergency before December 9.
- The candidate is a disorderly AI-debt and equity unwind, with hedge-fund deleveraging and impaired Treasury market functioning, the tail scenario the staff presented in October.
- Even then the first response would more likely be liquidity tools, with inflation at 3.8 percent.
- Essentially a crisis-only outcome.

**Cut of 25 bp**
- Same crisis logic in a milder form, or a political shock.
- Nothing in the data or in any participant's remarks supports easing; the lowest 2026 dot is the current rate.
- Near zero.

**Hold**
- Warsh has already shown he will pause after a hike when financial conditions are tightening on their own, and he will not be bound by dots or market pricing.
- Since October, AI equities have dropped, spreads and mortgage rates have risen, and banks' equity prices fell sharply.
- Oil has fallen on prospects of a US-Iran deal, which would remove the largest inflation driver.
- Real consumer spending stalled in October and the Beige Book is soft.
- The Financial Stability Report and several governors flag AI leverage as a vulnerability.
- Bowman explicitly weighs the costs of more tightening, and the President is publicly hostile to the Board's stance.
- A weak November jobs report on December 4, a sharper market selloff or a peace deal could let the Chairman argue that "the medicine is working" and wait until January.

**Hike of 25 bp**
- It is the SEP median and the stated expectation of "almost all participants" in the minutes.
- Markets and the Desk survey expected it, and some participants said they were comfortable with that pricing.
- October was explicitly framed as a wait for more information, and the information arrived on the hawkish side: core CPI and PCE up 0.3, twelve-month core PCE up to 3.3 percent, payrolls up 165,000 with upward revisions, GDP at 4.3 percent.
- Two voters already dissented for a hike. Waller and Barr have said they expect or support one, and Jefferson says inflation is the key signal.
- Not hiking would, as Waller argued, unwind priced-in tightening and loosen conditions, and would damage the "we act" credibility Warsh is building.
- A 25 bp step also fits the gradualism some participants favour while markets are fragile.

**Hike of 50 bp or more**
- Waller's own stated condition, core CPI around 0.3 or higher, was met. He said 25 bp alone "would put our credibility at risk".
- The October minutes record a couple of participants saying more than 25 bp could warrant consideration if inflation data were unfavourable.
- Headline PCE is 3.8 percent against an upper bound of 4.00 percent, so the real policy rate is roughly zero while GDP grows above 4 percent.
- Twelve-month core inflation is rising, not falling, and Cook warns of falling behind the curve.
- Warsh wants to show there is no "soft" target and could choose a decisive move after skipping October, particularly if the November jobs report is strong and markets stabilise.

## 4. What I am least sure of

- **The Chairman's reaction function.** Warsh gives no guidance, does not submit a dot and stresses financial conditions as the transmission channel.
  - This is the main reason hike25 is not above 85 percent.
  - It widens both tails: he could hold if markets are doing the tightening, or go 50 to make a point.
- **How far the November AI and credit selloff has gone, and where oil is now.**
  - The pack has only officials' qualitative remarks up to about November 20 and no market prices.
  - A serious deterioration would raise the chance of a hold and cut the chance of 50; stabilisation would do the reverse.
  - Current market-implied odds for December are not in the pack. The last reading, from the October meeting, was a "high" probability of 25 bp by year-end.
- **Data still to come before the meeting.** The November employment report is due December 4. November CPI comes out December 10, after the decision.
- **How much support Waller's 50 bp idea has.**
  - It is one governor plus "a couple" in the minutes.
  - Against that are a 10-2 October majority for no move, a couple who prefer gradualism and widespread financial-stability concern.
  - I treat 50 as a real but minority risk.
- **Pack coverage.** It contains no post-October remarks from Reserve Bank presidents (Williams, Logan, Kashkari, Paulson, Hammack), so the balance among voters is partly inferred from the minutes.

Net effect: the modal outcome of 25 bp is well supported, but I keep roughly a fifth of the probability on the two neighbouring outcomes. Hold is somewhat more likely than 50, because fragile markets and a gradualist majority weigh more heavily than one governor's call for a larger move. Cuts get only crisis-tail weight.
