Technical Notes
Changes in personal income and outlays for October

The October increase of $201.8 billion in personal income primarily reflected increases in compensation, government social benefits, and personal income receipts on assets.

Within compensation, the increase was led by private wages and salaries based on U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics’ (BLS) Current Employment Statistics (CES).
The increase in government social benefits was led by other social benefits, primarily reflecting one-time payments associated with the Medicare Improvement Fund premium rebate. For more information, refer to Medicare Improvement Fund Premium Rebate Frequently Asked Questions.
Within personal income receipts on assets, the increase was led by personal dividend income, based on data from publicly traded companies.
Revisions to personal income

Estimates have been updated for April through September. For April through June, estimates for compensation, personal taxes, and contributions for government social insurance reflect the incorporation of second-quarter wage and salary data from the BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages program. The estimates for July through September reflect updated BLS CES data.

GDP (Second Estimate) and Corporate Profits, 3rd Quarter 2026

Real gross domestic product (GDP) increased at an annual rate of 4.3 percent in the third quarter of 2026 (July, August, and September), according to the second estimate released today by the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA). In the second quarter, real GDP increased 2.2 percent.

The contributors to the increase in real GDP in the third quarter were increases in investment, consumer spending, and government spending that were partly offset by a decrease in exports. Imports, which are a subtraction in the calculation of GDP, increased.  

Real GDP was revised up 0.9 percentage point from the advance estimate, primarily reflecting upward revisions to investment and consumer spending. For more information, refer to the "Technical Notes" below.

Compared to the second quarter, the acceleration in real GDP in the third quarter primarily reflected an acceleration in investment and an upturn in government spending that were partly offset by a downturn in exports. Imports increased more in the third quarter than in the second quarter.

Real final sales to private domestic purchasers, the sum of consumer spending and gross private fixed investment, increased 5.8 percent in the third quarter, revised up 0.9 percentage point from the previous estimate.

The price index for gross domestic purchases increased 2.7 percent in the third quarter, revised down 0.3 percentage point from the previous estimate. The personal consumption expenditures (PCE) price index increased 2.1 percent, and the PCE price index, excluding food and energy increased 2.5 percent, both the same as previously estimated.

Real gross domestic income (GDI) increased 4.4 percent in the third quarter, compared with an increase of 2.3 percent (revised) in the second quarter. The average of real GDP and real GDI also increased 4.4 percent, compared with an increase of 2.2 percent (revised).

Profits from current production (corporate profits with inventory valuation and capital consumption adjustments) increased $206.0 billion in the third quarter, compared with an increase of $384.0 billion in the second quarter.

Technical Notes

Sources of revision to real GDP
Real GDP increased at an annual rate of 4.3 percent (1.1 percent at a quarterly rate 1 ) in the third quarter, an upward revision of 0.9 percentage point from the previous estimate, primarily reflecting upward revisions to investment and consumer spending.

The revision to investment was led by an upward revision to intellectual property products, notably software, based primarily on revisions to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics’ Producer Price Index for system and application software publishing, excluding maintenance, tech support and games.
The revision to consumer spending reflected an upward revision to goods that was partly offset by a downward revision to services.
Within goods, the upward revision was led by other nondurable goods (mainly pharmaceuticals), recreational goods and vehicles (mainly computer software and accessories), and gasoline and other energy goods, based on revised data from the U.S. Census Bureau Monthly Retail Trade Survey and new and revised data from the U.S. Department of Energy.
Within services, the largest contributor to the downward revision was health care (both hospital and nursing home services and outpatient services), based on newly available third-quarter U.S. Census Bureau Quarterly Services Survey data.
