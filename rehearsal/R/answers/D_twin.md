## The call

The question is descriptive, so the decision is which probability distribution to put on record. A forecast can be revised until it is fixed and scored after the 8-9 December meeting (derived: the October minutes, "the next meeting of the Committee would be held on Tuesday-Wednesday, December 8-9, 2026"). The number fixed at that point is the one-way step, and the cost of being wrong is the score it takes on the outcome that happens. The finding on timing is to decide now, and this version does: it commits on the material as it stands at 23 November and states, separately, how the number should move if later evidence is allowed.

This amended version puts 66 on a 25 basis point increase and 29 on a hold. That returns to the weights before the last revision, for different and narrower reasons.

**What is known.** The range is "3-3/4 to 4 percent", so the upper bound before the meeting is 4 percent, and the material records no action between meetings (Implementation Note, 28 October). The September projections put 4 participants at 4.375 for end-2026, 12 at 4.125 and 2 at 3.875. The October vote was 10 to 2, and both dissents "preferred to raise the target range for the federal funds rate by 1/4 percentage point". As of the October meeting, futures showed "high probabilities assigned to a 25 basis point hike by year-end", and the dealer-survey median expected "one rate hike in December and another one in March 2027". Some who favoured an increase in October "could have supported no change ... provided that it was clearly understood by market participants" (October minutes). Since then core CPI "rose 0.3 percent in October, as it did in September and August", payrolls "rose by 165,000", and on 20 November Governor Cook said "I preferred to raise the target range another 1/4 percentage point".

**What separates an increase from a hold.** Three items point to this meeting: the September projections, the October pricing and dealer survey, and the minutes' account of hike-leaning participants accepting the October hold once markets understood the step was later. The October hold matched the pricing, which placed the step "by year-end" and, in the dealer survey, in December. So the pricing is untested, not discounted, and it is still the one graded item that dates the step to this meeting.

What that pricing does not do is decide the hold. The proviso belongs to "some who favoured an increase". Ten voters held in October and two dissented anyway. A December hold needs a majority that still wants to wait, and it can absorb more dissents if pricing has not moved. The majority's stated reasons for waiting - "the durability of AI-related investment demand, the evolution of financing conditions, the transmission of the September rate increase to the economy, or geopolitical developments" - are open on the material. The one item since October that bears on them directly is financing conditions, and the material is split on its direction: tighter financial conditions are recorded, and Cook calls them "too accommodative". I give it a slight weight toward a hold, and no more.

October is therefore neither a Committee that defers by temperament nor one that holds only when markets let it. It was a majority deferral that hike-leaning participants accepted because the market already expected the step later.

The other items fit both outcomes and get no weight between them: the Chair's "I also don't believe in prejudging meetings", the oil decline, and the October inflation and jobs figures for the majority (they can move single votes; one October holder, Governor Bowman, now says "inflation pressures are persisting"). Governor Waller's "it is not necessary, for example, to raise rates at consecutive meetings" fits a December step after the October skip and does not address a second straight hold. The material grades the passage as justifying his own October vote, so I weigh it at near zero.

**Year-end versus December.** Both pricing and projections speak of year-end. The October minutes settle that no scheduled meeting falls before 8 December, and the question's terms record no action between meetings. Whether another scheduled meeting falls between 10 and 31 December is not in the material. From general knowledge, the Committee's calendar ends the year with its December meeting. That is an inference, not a sourced fact, and it needs a check against the published calendar. If it holds, year-end pricing and end-2026 projections are evidence about December itself, and the committed number leans on that reading. If a later 2026 meeting exists, the hold weight should go up toward 33.

**What stays unknowable from here.** Three things cannot be determined from the material: whether December pricing still stood when the pre-meeting quiet period began, what the November employment report (4 December) shows, and what the Chair and the six voters silent since October intend. I searched the supplied speeches through 20 November and found no public case for more time from any of them. The search's access was limited, because the window runs past the material's end. I count that absence for neither outcome. All it shows is that no voter had yet publicly argued for holding again.

**Pricing being wrong.** At 66/29, a hold costs a logarithmic score of -ln(0.29) = 1.24, and an increase scores -ln(0.66) = 0.42. This is my best estimate with no shading on it. Within the item-by-item ranges (57-71 on the increase, 24-36 on a hold, midpoints 64 and 30), it sits 2.0 above the increase midpoint and 1.0 below the hold midpoint. The reason it does not sit exactly on them is the year-end dating, if the calendar check holds, and the October and November data, which lean toward pricing having firmed rather than faded. The reason it sits no higher is that the hold has a majority path that does not need repricing, and financing conditions give that path some material. The age of the pricing widens these ranges; it does not move the centre. The tails stay thin. Every stated size preference is "1/4 percentage point". A search of the supplied material found no participant favouring a lower range (access: limited, because several forward-looking passages are blank in the copy). Whether a late shock opens a cut is unknown.

**The trigger.** Under a 23 November evidence cutoff (derived: the latest dated item in the material is 20 November, and the analysis is fixed on 23 November), none of the triggers below can fire before scoring, and option A stands as written. If later evidence is allowed, the deciding observations are December-increase pricing at the start of the quiet period, read for the middle zone and not only for crossing even odds, and any public case for more time from a voter silent since October.

The question record lists no override stakes.
