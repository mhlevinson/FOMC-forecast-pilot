# F03 - FOMC decision, 8-9 December 2026

Bottom line: a 25 basis point hike (upper bound 4.00 -> 4.25) is the clear base case at about three in four. The two real alternatives are a second consecutive hold and a 50 basis point hike, each around one in ten. Cuts are a remote tail.

Everything below comes from the evidence pack only (official documents captured 2026-11-23). Facts are stated as facts; my own inferences are marked "Inference".

## 1. What the Committee did and what it is signalling

Recent actions
- September 15-16: raised the target range 25 bp to 3.75-4.00 percent (Chairman Warsh: "we removed a dose of accommodation").
- October 27-28: held at 3.75-4.00 by a 10-2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike. The upper bound going into December is therefore 4.00.

Signals from the Committee's own documents
- September SEP dots for end-2026 (midpoint): 12 participants at 4.125, 4 at 4.375, 2 at 3.875. With the midpoint now 3.875 and only December left, the median implies exactly one more 25 bp hike in December; 16 of 18 had at least one more hike; 4 had two.
- October minutes: "almost all participants judged that ... further action to raise the target range - broadly consistent with the September Summary of Economic Projections - would likely be appropriate." "Some" participants (more than the two dissenting voters) favoured hiking in October; "a few" who supported the hold called it a close call. "A couple" said more than 25 bp could warrant consideration in December if inflation data were unfavourable. Some said market pricing of a December hike was consistent with their own path and that this alignment lowered the cost of waiting in October.
- Market pricing as reported in the minutes: futures assigned "high probabilities" to a 25 bp hike by year-end; the Desk survey median had one hike in December and another in March 2027.
- October statement: "The accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance ... The Committee will deliver price stability."

Signals from members since the meeting
- Waller (10 Nov, before the CPI release): would support 25 bp in December even if October inflation improved; if core CPI came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher" then "a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered," and "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk." He also argued that the October hold was acceptable only because the promised hikes must be delivered "in a reasonable period of time," otherwise policy loosens.
- Cook (20 Nov): dissenter; "waiting to act ... has a cost"; sceptical one hike is enough; data since September "fail to provide me with sufficient evidence that inflation is returning to a sustainable path."
- Barr (18 Nov): "I expect it will be appropriate to raise rates further."
- Jefferson (13 Nov): non-committal on timing, but "the trajectory of inflation [is] providing the more important signal."
- Bowman (10 Nov, after CPI): the most cautious voice. "There is still work to do," but "the benefit of additional policy tightening has to be carefully weighed against its costs for interest-rate-sensitive sectors"; stresses tighter financial conditions and recent market volatility.
- Warsh (press conference 28 Oct; remarks 16 Nov): refuses forward guidance and will not prejudge December; calls the interval "information rich"; says "We will deliver price stability - no ifs, ands, or buts"; says financial conditions are tighter but he is "unwilling and unable to call them 'tight'."

Inference: the centre of the Committee held in October in order to hike later with more information, not because it doubted more tightening was needed. December is the meeting the SEP, the minutes and market pricing all pointed to.

## 2. What the latest official data show

Inflation - pushes toward a hike, and toward a larger one at the margin
- October CPI: headline +0.4 percent m/m; core +0.3 percent for the third month running (Aug, Sep, Oct). Broad gains: shelter +0.3, transportation services +0.8, recreation +0.8, food away from home +0.5, motor vehicle insurance +0.7. Used cars -1.2 was the main offset. Energy rose only 0.7 after +5.2 in September.
- September PCE (BEA): headline +0.5 m/m, 3.6 percent y/y; core +0.3 m/m, 3.1 percent y/y. (Waller cites 3.7 percent for the headline 12-month figure; BEA's release says 3.6.)
- Governors' translations of the October CPI: Cook puts October PCE at 3.8 percent and core at 3.2 percent; Bowman puts core at 3.3 percent. Either way core PCE is moving up, not down.
- Core CPI at 0.3 is exactly the threshold Waller named for considering 50 bp.
- Beige Book (through 5 Oct): three Districts "robust" price growth, eight "moderate", four reporting acceleration; fuel surcharges passing through supply chains.

Employment - removes any labour-market reason to wait
- October payrolls +165,000 against a 12-month average of 48,000; August and September revised up by a combined 84,000. Unemployment 4.1 percent. Average hourly earnings +0.4 percent m/m, 3.1 percent y/y.
- Soft spots: part-time for economic reasons +339,000; long-term unemployed 27.6 percent of the unemployed; "low hire, low fire." These temper the picture but do not argue against a hike.

Activity - pushes toward a hike
- Q3 GDP (advance) +3.4 percent annualised; real final sales to private domestic purchasers +4.9 percent. Staff see output outpacing potential through 2028.
- Offsets: Beige Book describes only slight-to-modest growth in most Districts; real PCE was flat in September; housing and other rate-sensitive sectors are weak.

Financial conditions and shocks - the main force pulling toward a hold
- Minutes: a majority noted tighter financial conditions since September (mortgage rates, wider spreads for higher-risk and AI-related borrowers). Staff judged vulnerabilities could amplify shocks "somewhat more than usual."
- Mid-November: Barr refers to "the past couple of weeks" of investor questions about the AI boom; Bowman cites lower tech equity valuations, wider spreads, higher CDS on AI-linked firms.
- Oil: Brent above $110 in late October, then "dropping sharply" in early November on hopes of a US-Iran agreement (Bowman). A deal would lower headline inflation ahead and give doves a reason to look through energy.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

Cut of 50 bp or more
- Only in a disorderly financial accident before 9 December: an AI-related asset price collapse with forced deleveraging (the staff's tail scenario), compounded by the French sovereign stress, impairing market functioning. Even then the minutes show several participants insisting monetary policy is not the tool for financial stability and discussing separating market-function purchases from the policy stance. With PCE inflation near 3.8 percent this is a tail of a tail.

Cut of 25 bp
- Same family of scenarios, milder: a sharp equity and credit rout that visibly hits spending and hiring within two weeks, plus a collapse in oil on a Middle East settlement. No participant in the pack has voiced any case for easing; "almost all" see further hikes. Close to negligible.

Hold
- The Committee already chose to wait once despite hot data, and the Chairman will not pre-commit. Since October, financial conditions have tightened further, AI-linked assets have repriced, and oil has fallen on peace hopes. Bowman explicitly weighs the costs of further tightening and wants "greater clarity" on AI financing and geopolitics. Jefferson is non-committal. If the equity/credit sell-off deepens materially into December, or the November jobs report (4 Dec) and October PCE come in soft, a majority around the Chairman could decide the "monetary medicine" is working through markets and hold again while signalling a hike early in 2027. Warsh's framing that policy works "dominantly" through tightening financial conditions gives him a ready rationale.

Hike of 25 bp
- It is the SEP median, the minutes' "almost all participants" baseline, the market and Desk-survey expectation, and the stated plan of Waller (as a floor), Cook, Hammack and Barr. Every major data release since October points the same way: core CPI 0.3 for a third month, payrolls +165,000 with upward revisions, GDP 3.4 percent. Waller's logic binds the centre: the October hold was costless only if the priced-in hike is delivered; skipping it would ease conditions. A new SEP in December showing end-2026 below the September median without action would contradict "The Committee will deliver price stability." 25 bp also fits the "gradual approach" several participants favour and the majority's unease about financial stability.

Hike of 50 bp or more
- Waller's stated condition was met: core CPI was 0.3. He said 25 bp alone "would put our credibility at risk." "A couple" of participants already raised more than 25 bp for December; four September dots sat at 4.375, which now requires 50 bp; Cook and Hammack wanted to hike in October and Cook stresses getting "behind the curve." Having skipped October, 50 bp simply delivers the two hikes some participants thought appropriate for 2026. If October PCE shows core accelerating to 3.3 percent or more and November payrolls are strong again, the hawkish bloc (Waller, Cook, Hammack, plausibly Logan and Kashkari) could pull the Chairman, who wants to show there is "no soft inflation target."

## 4. What I am least sure of

- Market and financial-stability developments after mid-November. The pack hints at an AI-related sell-off but gives no magnitude. This is the main thing that could turn a hike into a hold, and it is not visible to me. It widens the hold probability.
- The Chairman. Warsh gives no forward guidance, does not submit a dot, and held in October against the lean of the data. His reaction function is the least documented piece. This widens both tails (hold and 50).
- How many others share Waller's 50 bp view. Only "a couple" raised it in October, the centre prefers gradualism, and financial conditions are tightening - Inference: 50 bp needs both further bad inflation data and calm markets, a narrow combination. This keeps 50 near one in ten rather than higher, despite Waller's condition being met.
- Data still to come before the meeting: October PCE and the November employment report (4 Dec). November CPI arrives on 10 Dec, after the decision. A weak jobs report shifts weight to hold; a hot PCE print shifts weight to 50.
- Geopolitics: a US-Iran agreement would pull energy prices down and soften the urgency; an escalation would do the reverse but also raise growth risks.
- The pack contains no speeches from Reserve Bank presidents after October, so the views of four voters (Williams, Kashkari, Logan, Paulson) plus Hammack are known only through the vote and the minutes.

Net effect: the direction (tightening) is well supported, so I keep about 86 percent on a hike of some size. The uncertainty is mostly about whether it slips again (hold) or is doubled (50), and I weight hold slightly above 50 because the Committee has just demonstrated its willingness to wait and because the visible new information since Waller's speech is mainly about tighter financial conditions.

- Hold: 13.0 percent
- Hike 25 bp: 76.0 percent
- Hike 50 bp or more: 10.0 percent
