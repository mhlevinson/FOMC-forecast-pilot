# F01 forecast: FOMC decision, 8-9 December 2026

Source: cp/K1/pack.md only (22 official documents, captured 2026-11-26T07:03:09Z). The upper bound in effect before the meeting is 4.00 percent (target range 3-3/4 to 4 percent).

## 1. What the Committee did recently and what it signals

**Recent actions**
- September 15-16: raised the range 25 bp to 3-3/4 to 4 percent, the first hike of this cycle ("removed a dose of accommodation").
- October 27-28: held, by a 10-2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike.
- The October statement said "the accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance" and "The Committee will deliver price stability."

**September SEP (18 participants, end-2026 midpoint)**
- 12 at 4.125 percent, which is one more 25 bp hike from the current 3.875 midpoint.
- 4 at 4.375 percent, which is 50 bp more.
- 2 at 3.875 percent, which is no further move.
- 17 of 18 saw inflation risks weighted to the upside.

**October minutes (published November 18)**
- "Almost all participants" judged that further action to raise the range, "broadly consistent with the September Summary of Economic Projections," would likely be appropriate.
- "Some" participants wanted a hike in October, and "a few" who supported the hold called it a close call.
- Some said market-implied tightening "in December and next year" matched their own view of the appropriate path.
- "A couple" said more than 25 bp could warrant consideration at the next meeting if inflation data were unfavourable.
- Futures at the time put a high probability on a 25 bp hike by year-end; the Desk survey median expected a December hike and another in March 2027.
- Arguments for holding in October were about timing: assess the September hike, AI investment durability, financing conditions and geopolitics. A couple favoured a "gradual approach."

**Members since the meeting**
- Waller (voter, November 10, before the CPI release): would support 25 bp in December even if October inflation improved. If core CPI was "around 0.3 percent or even higher," a 50 bp hike "should be considered," and "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk." He also said promised hikes must be carried out or financial conditions will loosen.
- Cook (voter, November 20): repeated her case for hiking, called waiting costly, and said the data since September give no evidence of disinflation. She did not call for 50 bp.
- Barr (voter, November 18): expects "it will be appropriate to raise rates further."
- Jefferson (voter, November 13): inflation is "the more important signal" for policy; he did not commit to a date.
- Bowman (voter, November 10): "still work to do" on inflation, but the benefit of more tightening "has to be carefully weighed against its costs for interest-rate-sensitive sectors," and she is watching market volatility. She is the most cautious voice in the pack.
- Chairman Warsh (October 28 press conference): refuses forward guidance and will not prejudge December, but said the coming period is "information rich" and that "we don't just talk—we act."

Net signal: a hike in December is the expected path. The live question is 25 bp versus 50 bp, with a smaller chance of another hold.

## 2. Latest official data and which way each pushes

**Inflation: pushes toward a hike, with some weight on a larger one**
- October CPI: all items +0.4 percent; core +0.3 percent for the third straight month. This meets Waller's stated condition for considering 50 bp.
- October PCE prices: headline +0.3 percent on the month (down from 0.5) and 3.8 percent over 12 months; core +0.3 percent on the month and 3.3 percent over 12 months (up from 3.1 in September).
- Breadth is the concern: transportation services +0.8 percent, recreation +0.8, food away from home +0.5, hospital services +0.8.
- Beige Book: prices rose moderately, wage growth ticked up to moderate, and contacts expect costs and prices to keep rising.
- Mitigating points: the monthly headline rate slowed, energy rose only 0.7 percent after 5.2, Brent fell sharply in early November from above $110, and the third-quarter PCE price index was 2.1 percent annualised (core 2.5).

**Employment: no obstacle to a hike**
- October payrolls +165,000, with August and September revised up by 84,000 combined.
- Unemployment 4.1 percent; average hourly earnings +0.4 percent on the month and 3.1 percent over 12 months.
- Soft spots: involuntary part-time work rose 339,000, long-term unemployed are 27.6 percent of the total, and hiring rates are low.

**Activity: mixed, strong in hindsight and softer at the margin**
- Third-quarter GDP revised up to 4.3 percent from 3.4; real final sales to private domestic purchasers 5.8 percent. This pushes toward a hike.
- October real PCE was flat, and the saving rate was 4.1 percent.
- Beige Book: eight Districts with slight-to-moderate growth and four flat; consumer spending little changed; housing, construction and autos weaker.
- Financial conditions have tightened: lower AI equity valuations, wider credit spreads, higher mortgage rates. The November Financial Stability Report and several speeches flag AI-debt fragility. This argues against going faster than 25 bp, and in a stress scenario against moving at all.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

- **Cut of 50 bp or more:** only a sudden financial crisis before December 9 would do it, such as a disorderly AI-credit unwind with fire sales and impaired market functioning (the tail the staff described in October). Even then the first response would more likely be liquidity tools, with inflation at 3.8 percent.
- **Cut of 25 bp:** the same shock in milder form, combined with collapsing oil prices and a very weak November jobs report on December 4. Nobody in the pack advocates easing, and none of the 18 September projections had a year-end rate below the current level.
- **Hold:** Warsh rejects forward guidance and held in October despite a hike being justifiable. Conditions have tightened on their own, oil has fallen, October real spending was flat, Bowman stresses costs to rate-sensitive sectors, and the President has criticised the Board. A further equity or credit selloff, or a weak jobs report, could let the Committee argue that the market is doing its work.
- **Hike of 25 bp:** this is the path "almost all participants" described, the SEP median for year-end, and what markets and the Desk survey expected. October's pause was explicitly to gather information, and that information (core CPI 0.3, core PCE 3.3 percent, GDP 4.3 percent, payrolls 165,000) confirms the case. Waller, Barr, Cook and Hammack are already there, and Jefferson leans that way. A quarter point fits the "dose" language and the gradualism in the minutes, and respects the financial-stability worries.
- **Hike of 50 bp or more:** Waller's trigger was met exactly. A couple of participants raised the possibility in October, four September dots implied 50 bp more by year-end, and core PCE inflation is rising. Having skipped October, 50 bp would make up the lost step, and a Chair who promises price stability "no ifs, ands, or buts" might want a decisive move.

## 4. What I am least sure of

- **25 bp versus 50 bp is the biggest uncertainty.** Waller's condition was met, but only he has said so publicly. Cook, Barr, Jefferson and Bowman all spoke after or on the day of the CPI release and none endorsed 50 bp. Core PCE held at 0.3 percent without accelerating, and headline slowed. A 50 bp move would exceed what markets priced in October, at a time of visible fragility in AI credit. I keep 50 bp as a real tail, not the base case.
- **Warsh's reaction function.** He has chaired only a handful of meetings, gives no guidance, and already surprised some observers by holding in October. This widens both tails, hold and 50 bp, relative to a normal well-telegraphed meeting.
- **Events not in the pack.** The November jobs report (December 4), market moves after November 25, and Middle East developments could shift the outcome. The pack has no market pricing later than the October minutes. This keeps the hold probability from being very small.
- **Composition of the Committee.** A White House proceeding concerning Cook is mentioned; a change there would not alter the majority.

These points keep my 25 bp probability well below what a clearly signalled meeting would normally earn, with the remainder spread to 50 bp first and hold second. Cuts stay at token levels.
