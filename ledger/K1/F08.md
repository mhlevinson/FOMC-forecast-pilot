# F08 forecast: FOMC decision, 8-9 December 2026

Basis: the evidence pack cp/K1/pack.md only (22 official documents, captured 2026-11-26). The upper bound in effect before the meeting is 4.00 percent (target range 3-3/4 to 4 percent).

## 1. What the Committee did and what it signals

**Recent actions**
- September 15-16: raised the range 25 bp to 3-3/4 to 4 percent. Chairman Warsh described it as removing "a dose of accommodation".
- October 27-28: held, by a 10-2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike.
- The October statement said "the accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance" and "The Committee will deliver price stability."

**September SEP**
- The median end-2026 funds rate is 4.1 percent, one more 25 bp hike from the current range.
- The dots: 12 of 18 at 4.125, 4 at 4.375, 2 at 3.875. So 16 of 18 participants wrote down at least one more hike by year-end, and December is the last meeting of the year.

**October minutes (released November 18)**
- "Almost all participants" judged that further increases "broadly consistent with the September Summary of Economic Projections" would likely be appropriate.
- "Some" participants wanted a hike in October, and "a few" who supported holding called it a close call.
- Several saw market pricing of a December hike as what made the October hold low-risk.
- "A couple" said more than 25 bp could warrant consideration in December if inflation data were unfavourable.
- "A couple" favoured a gradual approach to firming.
- Futures put a high probability on a 25 bp hike by year-end, and the Desk survey median expected a December hike.

**Speeches since the meeting**
- Waller (November 10): would support 25 bp in December even if October inflation improved. If core CPI came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher", 50 bp "should be considered" and "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk". He also argued that promised hikes must actually be carried out or conditions will loosen.
- Cook (November 20): feels "urgency", is "skeptical" that one 25 bp hike is enough, and says the data since September give no evidence that inflation is returning to a sustainable path.
- Barr (November 18): "I expect it will be appropriate to raise rates further."
- Jefferson (November 13): inflation is "the more important signal", with persistent price pressures.
- Bowman (November 10): the most cautious. "Still work to do", but the benefit of more tightening "has to be carefully weighed against its costs", and she is watching financial conditions and market volatility.
- Warsh: rejects forward guidance and will not prejudge the meeting. He called the interval "information rich" and stressed that tighter financial conditions show "policy is working". His November 16 remarks carried no policy signal.

Overall, the Committee has all but pre-committed to a 25 bp hike in December. A vocal minority is open to 50 bp, and the Chairman is deliberately non-committal.

## 2. Latest data

**Inflation pushes toward a hike, with some push toward a larger one**
- October CPI: headline +0.4 percent and core +0.3 percent, the third straight 0.3 for core. That meets Waller's stated trigger for considering 50 bp.
- October PCE prices: +0.3 percent headline and +0.3 percent core on the month. The 12-month rates are 3.8 percent headline and 3.3 percent core, up from 3.7 and 3.1 in September.
- These are roughly on the September SEP track for 2026 (3.7 headline, 3.4 core), so they are bad but not a clear upside surprise against the Committee's own projections.
- The Beige Book reports moderate price increases, with firms expecting costs and prices to keep rising.
- Mitigating: Brent fell sharply in early November on hopes of a US-Iran agreement, the October energy CPI rose only 0.7 percent after 5.2 percent, and tariff effects are waning.

**Employment gives no reason to wait**
- October payrolls rose 165,000, with 84,000 of upward revisions to the prior two months.
- Unemployment is 4.1 percent.
- Average hourly earnings rose 0.4 percent on the month and 3.1 percent over 12 months.
- The four-week average of initial claims is about 195,000.
- The softer notes are low hiring, a long-term unemployed share of 27.6 percent and a 339,000 rise in involuntary part-time work.

**Activity pushes toward a hike**
- Q3 real GDP was revised up to 4.3 percent, and final sales to private domestic purchasers rose 5.8 percent.
- Softer at the margin: October real PCE was flat, and the Beige Book has eight Districts with slight to moderate growth and four flat, with housing, construction and autos weak.

**Financial conditions are the main argument for caution**
- Conditions have tightened: lower AI-related equity prices, wider high-yield and AI credit spreads, higher mortgage rates and a stronger dollar.
- The Financial Stability Report and Barr's speech point to an AI-equity wobble in "the past couple of weeks".
- Other stress points are the repricing of French sovereign debt and the Binance cyber incident.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

**Cut of 50 bp or more**
- Only an acute crisis before December 9 would do it, such as a disorderly AI-equity and credit collapse with fire sales and market dysfunction, forcing an emergency reversal.
- Even then, with core PCE at 3.3 percent, liquidity tools would come first.

**Cut of 25 bp**
- The same crisis scenario in milder form, or a sudden collapse in activity and oil prices.
- No participant has hinted at easing. The lowest September dots (3.875) correspond to the current range.

**Hold**
- Warsh has already held once against expectations of steady tightening. He rejects calendar-based policy and says tightening financial conditions are doing the work.
- A deeper AI-equity selloff, wider credit spreads, a US-Iran deal that sends oil sharply lower, or a weak November jobs report (due December 4) could let the centre argue for more time.
- Bowman's cost-benefit framing and Barr's and the minutes' financial-stability concerns provide cover.
- Political pressure from the White House is visible in the press-conference record.

**Hike of 25 bp**
- It is the SEP median and the "almost all participants" baseline in the minutes.
- The October hold was explicitly justified by an expected December move.
- Waller's minimum is 25 bp, two voters already dissented for it, and Barr and Jefferson lean that way.
- Markets and the Desk survey expect it.
- Inflation, jobs and GDP all support it, and it is consistent with the gradualism some participants want.
- Not delivering would loosen conditions and damage the "we will deliver price stability" message.

**Hike of 50 bp or more**
- Waller's trigger of core CPI around 0.3 was met, and he tied credibility to 50 bp.
- Cook and Hammack want faster tightening, and "a couple" flagged a larger move in October.
- Four dots already had 4.375 for year-end, which now requires 50 bp.
- GDP at 4.3 percent, payrolls at 165,000 and core PCE rising to 3.3 percent can be read as a positive, widening output gap.
- A 50 bp move would simply make up for the skipped October hike.
- Warsh's "we don't just talk—we act" rhetoric leaves room for a forceful step.

## 4. What I am least sure of

- **Warsh's reaction function.** He is new, refuses guidance, and surprised the hawks in October. That widens both the hold tail and the 50 bp tail relative to a normal "priced-in" meeting.
- **Events not yet in the pack.** The November jobs report (December 4), financial-market developments in the AI complex and a possible US-Iran deal could all move the outcome. The pack gives no market pricing after the October CPI, so I cannot tell how much 50 bp is priced. Tail risks here mostly favour hold over 50 bp, because market stress argues for caution.
- **How many share Waller's view on 50 bp.** October PCE roughly matched the SEP path, the centre (Williams, Jefferson, Powell, Paulson, Barr) has shown no appetite for more than 25 bp, and Bowman is hesitant. That narrows the 50 bp probability.
- **Net effect.** 25 bp remains strongly modal, but I leave a combined 26 percent or so on hold and 50 bp, with hold slightly larger. Cuts get token weight.
