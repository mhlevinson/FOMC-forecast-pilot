# F04 - FOMC 8-9 December 2026: change in the upper bound of the target range

Source: evidence pack only (/home/claude/cp/R/pack.md, 21 official documents, cutoff 2026-11-23T07:06:35Z). Facts are labelled as stated in the pack; my own readings are labelled as inference.

## 1. What the Committee did and what it signals

Facts from the pack:
- September 15-16: the Committee raised the range by 25 bp to 3-3/4 to 4 percent (Cook speech, minutes: "the September increase in the target range").
- October 27-28: held at 3-3/4 to 4 percent on a 10-2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike. Upper bound in effect before the December meeting: 4.00 percent.
- October statement: "Inflation remains elevated. The accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent goal. The Committee will deliver price stability."
- October minutes: "almost all participants judged that... further action to raise the target range... would likely be appropriate." "Some" already wanted a hike in October. "A few" who supported the hold called it "a close call". "A couple" said "an increase of more than 25 basis points could warrant consideration at the next meeting if incoming inflation data were unfavorable." "Some" said market-implied tightening "in December and next year" matched their own view of the appropriate path.
- Market read reported in the minutes: futures assigned "high probabilities" to a 25 bp hike by year-end; the Desk survey median expected "one rate hike in December and another one in March 2027".
- September SEP dots for end-2026: 2 participants at 3.875 (no further hike), 12 at 4.125 (one more 25 bp), 4 at 4.375 (two more). December is the last meeting of 2026, so 16 of 18 needed at least +25 bp in December to hit their own September projection, and 4 would need +50.
- Speeches after the meeting:
  - Waller (Nov 10, before CPI): "even if... the monthly rate of inflation improves in October, I am leaning toward supporting an increase in our policy rate of 25 basis points at the FOMC's December meeting." If core CPI came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher... a 50 basis point hike in the policy rate should be considered... Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk." He also argued the October hold was only acceptable because promised hikes "are eventually carried out".
  - Barr (Nov 18): "I expect it will be appropriate to raise rates further."
  - Cook (Nov 20): dissenter; "waiting to act... has a cost"; data since September "fail to provide me with sufficient evidence that inflation is returning to a sustainable path."
  - Jefferson (Nov 13): "I see the trajectory of inflation as providing the more important signal"; no explicit December commitment.
  - Bowman (Nov 10): the most cautious voice - "the benefit of additional policy tightening has to be carefully weighed against its costs for interest-rate-sensitive sectors", but also "there is still work to do to bring inflation closer to 2 percent."
  - Warsh (Oct 28 press conference): refuses forward guidance ("I also don't believe in prejudging meetings"), but: "we don't just talk - we act", "inflation is too high", and the coming interval is "information rich".

Inference: the Committee is in a tightening phase, paused once, and its own documents point to a hike in December as the base case. The October hold was sold as a matter of timing, not direction. The October core CPI print (0.3) literally meets Waller's stated trigger for putting 50 bp on the table.

## 2. Latest official data and which way they push

Inflation - pushes toward a hike, and toward the larger-hike tail:
- October CPI: all items +0.4 percent m/m (after +0.6); core +0.3 percent, "as it did in September and August"; shelter +0.3, food +0.5, transportation services +0.8, recreation +0.8.
- September PCE: +0.5 m/m, 3.6 percent y/y; core +0.3 m/m, 3.1 percent y/y.
- Officials' October PCE estimates: headline 3.8 percent, core 3.2 percent (Cook); core 3.3 percent (Bowman). So core is still rising on a 12-month basis and broadening (Waller: over 70 percent of core services components above 3 percent).
- Beige Book (through Oct 5): eight Districts moderate price growth, three robust; "four Districts reported that the pace of price increases picked up."
- Offsets: tariff effects waning; Brent fell "sharply" in early November on hopes of a US-Iran agreement (Bowman); October gasoline CPI only +1.0 after +9.1.

Employment - pushes toward a hike (no labor-market reason to wait):
- October payrolls +165,000, with +84,000 of upward revisions to August-September; unemployment 4.1 percent; average hourly earnings +0.4 percent m/m, 3.1 percent y/y.
- Soft spots: part-time for economic reasons +339,000; long-term unemployed 27.6 percent of the unemployed; "low hire, low fire".

Activity - pushes toward a hike:
- Q3 GDP +3.4 percent annualized; real final sales to private domestic purchasers +4.9 percent.
- Offsets: real PCE flat in September, real DPI -0.3 percent; saving rate 3.8 percent; housing and other rate-sensitive sectors weak; Beige Book describes mostly slight-to-modest growth.

Financial conditions - the main brake:
- Minutes: a majority saw tightening since September; high-yield and AI-related spreads wider; bank equities down sharply; French sovereign stress.
- Barr (Nov 16) refers to investor doubts about the AI boom "these past couple of weeks"; Bowman notes tech equity valuations "dropped". Cook, by contrast, still judges financial conditions "too accommodative".

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

- Cut of 50 bp or more: only a severe, fast-moving financial accident before December 9 - a disorderly AI-related repricing with hedge-fund deleveraging and impaired market functioning, the tail scenario the staff described at the October meeting. Even then the first response would more likely be liquidity tools than a rate cut with core inflation above 3 percent.
- Cut of 25 bp: the same accident in milder form, combined with a collapse in oil after a Middle East settlement and a sharply negative November jobs report. Nothing in the pack points here; no participant is on record as contemplating a cut.
- Hold: the October pattern repeats. Warsh will not prejudge; the majority already chose "accumulation of more information" once; Bowman is openly weighing the cost of more tightening; financial conditions have tightened and AI credit is wobbling; oil has dropped; "almost all" expect further hikes "in the months ahead", which does not have to mean December. A soft October PCE (Nov 25) or November jobs report (Dec 4), or renewed market stress, would give the centre cover to wait until January.
- Hike of 25 bp: the base case on every official signal. Sixteen of eighteen September dots imply it; "almost all" participants expect further hikes; markets and the Desk survey expect December; Waller, Barr and Cook are explicit; Jefferson leans the same way; two dissenters already wanted it in October; data since then (core CPI 0.3 for a third month, payrolls +165,000, GDP 3.4 percent) removed the reasons for waiting. Waller's argument that undelivered hikes would loosen conditions gives the Committee a reason not to hold twice.
- Hike of 50 bp or more: Waller's stated condition (core CPI around 0.3) was met; "a couple" of participants flagged more than 25 bp in the minutes; four September dots require +50 by year-end; "a few" warned that delay could force "a larger and more disruptive tightening later"; having skipped October, 50 bp would put the path back on the September SEP track; and a Chair who rejects forward guidance is freer to surprise.

## 4. What I am least sure of

- The Chair's own reaction function (inference). Warsh hiked in September and held in October with a hawkish script. I cannot tell from the pack whether the October hold reflected genuine gradualism, pre-election caution he denies, or sensitivity to financial conditions. This is the main reason hold stays in the mid-teens rather than single digits.
- How many participants share Waller's 50 bp view. The minutes say "a couple"; Cook, the most hawkish governor on record, did not ask for 50 in her November 20 speech; the centre spoke of a "gradual approach". That keeps hike50 near one in nine rather than one in four.
- Unseen data and events: October PCE (Nov 25), November employment (Dec 4), the path of oil and any US-Iran agreement, and AI-related equity and credit markets. These widen both tails - soft data or market stress toward hold, another hot print toward 50.
- No market pricing after the October minutes is in the pack, and no Reserve Bank president speeches are included, so my read of the regional presidents rests on the minutes and the dots alone.

Net effect: these uncertainties widen the distribution around 25 bp on both sides but do not move the mode. Cuts stay at token levels because inflation, employment and activity all point the other way.
