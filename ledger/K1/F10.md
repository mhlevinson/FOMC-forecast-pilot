# F10 forecast: FOMC decision, 8-9 December 2026

Most likely outcome: a 25 basis point hike, taking the range from 3-3/4–4 percent to 4–4-1/4 percent. I put that at about 77 percent, with a hold as the main alternative and a 50 basis point hike as a real but smaller tail.

## 1. What the Committee did and what it signals

**Recent actions**
- September 2026: raised the range 25 bp to 3-3/4–4 percent.
- October 27-28: held, by a 10–2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike.
- The October statement said "the accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy stance" and "The Committee will deliver price stability."

**September projections (SEP)**
- Of 18 participants, 12 put the end-2026 midpoint at 4.125 percent, 4 at 4.375 percent and 2 at 3.875 percent.
- The current midpoint is 3.875 percent, so 16 of 18 projected at least one more hike by year-end. December is the only 2026 meeting left.

**October minutes**
- "Almost all participants" judged that further increases, "broadly consistent with the September Summary of Economic Projections", would likely be appropriate.
- "Some" participants already favoured hiking in October, and "a few" who supported the hold called it a close call.
- Some said market pricing of tightening "in December and next year" matched their own view of the appropriate path.
- "A couple" said more than 25 bp could warrant consideration in December if inflation data were unfavourable.
- Futures put a high probability on a 25 bp hike by year-end, and the Desk survey median expected a December hike.

**Members since the meeting**
- Waller (Nov 10): hikes are needed "sooner rather than later" and promised hikes must be carried out in a reasonable time. He would support 25 bp in December even if October CPI improved. If core CPI came in around 0.3 percent or higher, 50 bp "should be considered".
- Barr (Nov 18): expects "it will be appropriate to raise rates further".
- Cook (Nov 20): still urgent; data since September "fail to provide me with sufficient evidence" of disinflation.
- Jefferson (Nov 13): the inflation trajectory is "the more important signal"; no commitment on timing.
- Bowman (Nov 10): the most cautious. "There is still work to do", but the benefit of more tightening "has to be carefully weighed against its costs for interest-rate-sensitive sectors", and financial conditions have tightened.
- Warsh: refuses forward guidance, but said "we don't just talk—we act" and called the intermeeting period "information rich".

Known or likely hike supporters among voters are Cook, Hammack, Waller and Barr, with Jefferson leaning that way. The pack has no speeches from Reserve Bank presidents, so the other voters are inferred from the SEP and minutes.

## 2. Latest data

**Inflation pushes toward a hike, with some support for 50 bp**
- October CPI: headline +0.4 percent, core +0.3 percent for the third straight month. That meets the condition Waller set for considering 50 bp.
- October PCE prices: headline +0.3 percent on the month and 3.8 percent on the year. Core was +0.3 percent and 3.3 percent on the year, up from 3.1 percent in September.
- Breadth is widening across services, recreation, medical care, insurance and food away from home.
- The Beige Book reports moderate price increases, input costs outpacing selling prices, and wage growth picking up to "moderate".
- Two offsets: energy rose only 0.7 percent in October after 5.2 percent, and Brent fell sharply in early November on hopes of a US–Iran deal. Core PCE at 3.3 percent is close to the SEP median of 3.4 percent, so this is persistence more than a fresh upside shock.

**Employment pushes toward a hike, or at least removes any reason to wait**
- October payrolls rose 165,000, with 84,000 of upward revisions to the prior two months.
- Unemployment is 4.1 percent, and the 4-week average of initial claims is about 195,000.
- Average hourly earnings rose 0.4 percent on the month and 3.1 percent on the year.
- Softer details: low hiring, long-term unemployed at 27.6 percent of the total, and involuntary part-time work up 339,000.

**Activity pushes toward a hike, with caveats**
- Q3 GDP was revised up to 4.3 percent, and real final sales to private domestic purchasers to 5.8 percent.
- Real PCE was flat in October, and the Beige Book shows consumer spending little changed, with housing and construction weakening.
- Financial conditions have tightened. AI equities fell and credit spreads widened in early-to-mid November.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

- **Cut of 50 bp or more:** only a sudden systemic event would do it, such as a disorderly AI debt and equity unwind with hedge fund fire sales, a cyber incident, or a sovereign stress spillover. The October financial stability discussion describes these channels, but nothing in the pack shows one under way.
- **Cut of 25 bp:** the same shock in milder form, combined with a collapse in oil prices and a very weak November jobs report. This is very remote with core PCE at 3.3 percent and almost all participants expecting to tighten.
- **Hold:** the October logic could repeat. Financial conditions have tightened further, AI investment is being repriced, oil has dropped on peace hopes, real spending stalled in October, and rate-sensitive sectors are weak. Bowman voices this view, Warsh dislikes being boxed in, and a weak jobs report on December 4 or sharper market stress could tip a divided Committee.
- **Hike of 25 bp:** this is the Committee's own baseline. It matches the SEP, the "almost all" language in the minutes, the market and Desk survey expectations, and explicit statements from Waller, Barr and Cook. The data since October are firmly on the hawkish side, and Waller argues that failing to deliver would loosen conditions and cost credibility.
- **Hike of 50 bp or more:** Waller's stated trigger was met, "a couple" of participants flagged a larger move, four participants had two more hikes pencilled in for 2026, and growth is running above 4 percent. Having skipped October, the Committee could choose to catch up in one step.

## 4. What I am least sure of

- **The centre of the Committee.** I have no post-meeting views from Warsh, Williams, Powell or the voting Reserve Bank presidents. Warsh rejects guidance, and his "appropriate protocol" language is ambiguous. This widens both the hold and the 50 bp tails.
- **Financial markets since mid-November.** Barr and Bowman describe an AI-related selloff and wider spreads. I do not know market pricing after the CPI release and Waller's speech, or how severe the stress has become. This is the main route to a hold.
- **Data still to come.** The November jobs report arrives on December 4. November CPI comes on December 10, after the decision. A weak jobs number is the other route to a hold.
- **Appetite for 50 bp.** October core PCE was not a clear upside surprise relative to the SEP, oil has eased, and most participants prefer a gradual path, so I keep 50 bp below 10 percent.
- **Political pressure.** The pack records the President criticising the Board, which adds a little to the hold tail.

Together these keep me from going much above the high 70s on a 25 bp hike, despite very strong signalling.

## 5. Probabilities

- Cut of 50 bp or more: 0.2 percent
- Cut of 25 bp: 0.6 percent
- Hold: 13.2 percent
- Hike of 25 bp: 77.0 percent
- Hike of 50 bp or more: 9.0 percent
