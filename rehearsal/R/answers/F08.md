# F08 - FOMC 8-9 December 2026: change in the upper bound of the target range

Bottom line: a 25 basis point hike (upper bound 4.00 -> 4.25 percent) is the clear base case. The two live alternatives are a second consecutive hold and a 50 basis point hike. Cuts are a remote tail.

Everything below is drawn only from the evidence pack (21 official documents, captured 2026-11-23). Facts are stated as facts; my own readings are marked "Inference".

## 1. What the Committee did recently and what it signals about December

Recent actions (facts)
- September 15-16: raised the range 25 bp to 3-3/4 to 4 percent. Chairman Warsh called it removing "a dose of accommodation".
- October 27-28: held at 3-3/4 to 4 percent on a 10-2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented in favor of a 25 bp hike. The upper bound in effect before the December meeting is therefore 4.00 percent.
- The October statement tied the hold to "the accumulation of more information" and added "The Committee will deliver price stability."

Signals from the Committee's own documents (facts)
- September SEP dots for end-2026: 12 participants at 4.125, 4 at 4.375, 2 at 3.875. So 16 of 18 projected at least one more 25 bp hike by year-end, and December is the last meeting of the year.
- October minutes: "almost all participants" judged that further action to raise the range, "broadly consistent with the September Summary of Economic Projections", would likely be appropriate. "Some" participants (more than the two voting dissenters) favored hiking in October. "A few" who supported the hold called it a close call. "Some" said market-implied tightening "in December and next year" matched their own view of the appropriate path, and several saw that alignment as what made holding in October low-risk. "A couple" said a move larger than 25 bp could warrant consideration in December if inflation data were unfavorable.
- Minutes on market pricing at the time of the October meeting: futures put a high probability on a 25 bp hike by year-end; the Desk survey median expected a hike in December and another in March 2027.
- Warsh (October 28 press conference): refuses forward guidance and "prejudging meetings", but described the coming period as "information rich", said "we don't just talk - we act", and said inflation "is too high". He also said tighter financial conditions are the main channel and that he is "unwilling and unable to call them tight".

Signals from individual members since the meeting (facts)
- Waller (Nov 10, before the CPI release that day): held in October only because hikes were already priced; "ensuring that the promised hikes are implemented in a reasonable period of time" matters. He would support 25 bp in December even if October CPI improved. If core CPI came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher", a 50 bp hike "should be considered" and "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk".
- Cook (Nov 20): dissenter; "waiting to act ... has a cost"; skeptical one hike is enough; post-September inflation data "fail to provide me with sufficient evidence" of progress.
- Barr (Nov 18): "I expect it will be appropriate to raise rates further" if the economy evolves as he forecasts.
- Jefferson (Nov 13): data since October show "persistent price pressures"; "the trajectory of inflation" is "the more important signal" for policy. No explicit December commitment.
- Bowman (Nov 10): inflation pressures "persisting", labor market "firming", "still work to do". But she also says the benefit of more tightening "has to be carefully weighed against its costs for interest-rate-sensitive sectors", stresses that financial conditions have tightened, and wants clarity on AI financing and geopolitics.

Inference: the October hold was explicitly a timing decision inside an agreed tightening path, not a pause in that path. The institutional weight (SEP, minutes, market pricing the Committee itself endorsed, and at least four governors on record) points at a hike in December. The only speaker in the pack who leans toward patience is Bowman, and even she frames the data as hawkish.

## 2. What the latest official data show

Inflation - pushes toward a hike (and is the only thing that could push toward 50)
- October CPI (Nov 10): headline +0.4 percent m/m after +0.6; core +0.3 percent for the third straight month. Energy +0.7 after +5.2. Shelter +0.3, food +0.5, transportation services +0.8, recreation +0.8, hospital services +0.8. Broad-based.
- September PCE (Oct 29): headline +0.5 m/m, 3.6 percent y/y; core +0.3 m/m, 3.1 percent y/y.
- Officials' translation of October CPI into PCE: headline about 3.8 percent y/y, core 3.2 percent (Cook) to 3.3 percent (Bowman). That is at or above the September SEP's 2026 medians (3.7 headline, 3.4 core) and moving the wrong way.
- Waller's stated trigger for considering 50 bp (core CPI around 0.3 or higher) was met by the release. Inference: core held steady rather than accelerating, and headline and energy decelerated, so this reads as "bad but not worse" - enough to lock in 25, not obviously enough to move the center of the Committee to 50.
- October Beige Book: prices rising moderately to robustly in 11 of 12 Districts, with fuel surcharges passing through.

Employment - pushes toward a hike (removes the main reason to wait)
- October payrolls +165,000, with August and September revised up a combined 84,000. Unemployment 4.1 percent. Average hourly earnings +0.4 percent m/m, 3.1 percent y/y.
- Soft spots: part time for economic reasons +339,000; long-term unemployed 27.6 percent of the unemployed; "low hire, low fire". These temper the picture but officials uniformly call the labor market stable or firming and at maximum employment.

Activity - pushes toward a hike
- Q3 GDP advance +3.4 percent; real final sales to private domestic purchasers +4.9 percent. Staff see growth above potential through 2028.
- Offsets: real PCE flat in September and real disposable income -0.3 percent; saving rate 3.8 percent; housing and other rate-sensitive sectors weak; Beige Book activity only slight to modest in most Districts.

Financial conditions and outside risks - the main push toward a hold
- Minutes: a majority noted tighter financial conditions since September; credit spreads wider, especially AI-related high yield; bank equities down sharply; French sovereign stress.
- Barr (Nov 16) refers to investor questions about the AI boom "these past couple of weeks"; Bowman notes tech valuations dropped and CDS widened. The November Financial Stability Report flags AI-related leverage and more than $3 trillion of off-balance-sheet obligations. Waller's footnote cites a record cyber heist at Binance on October 30.
- Oil: Brent above $110 in late October, then "dropping sharply" in early November on hopes of a US-Iran agreement (Bowman).

Still to come before the decision: October PCE and the second Q3 GDP estimate (Nov 25), the November employment report (Dec 4), a Beige Book. November CPI arrives Dec 10, after the meeting.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

Cut of 50 bp or more
- Would need a disorderly financial event before Dec 9 - an AI-debt and equity unwind turning into fire sales, a funding-market seizure, or a cyber event that impairs market functioning - severe enough for the Committee to drop the inflation fight inside two weeks. The staff's financial stability work describes the amplification channels. With inflation near 3.8 percent and the Committee discussing asset purchases for market functioning as separate from monetary accommodation, even a crisis would more likely be met with liquidity tools and a hold than with a rate cut. Remote.

Cut of 25 bp
- Same family of scenarios in milder form, or a collapse in the November jobs report combined with a market rout. Nothing in the pack shows any participant contemplating a cut; the lowest 2026 dot is no change. Remote.

Hold (no change)
- The Committee already chose to wait once when "almost all" expected to hike later, so its revealed preference under Warsh is to be patient at the margin. Warsh says financial conditions are the dominant channel and that markets are doing some of the work. Since October, AI-related equity and credit have repriced further, the FSR has put AI leverage in the spotlight, and oil has fallen sharply on peace hopes. Bowman explicitly weighs the costs of further tightening and wants more clarity on AI financing and geopolitics - both things that will not be resolved by Dec 9. If markets deteriorate further or a US-Iran deal lands and energy prices drop, a majority could argue for one more meeting of information, especially with a new SEP and dots available to signal that hikes are still coming. Political pressure from the White House is in the background.

Hike of 25 bp
- It is what the Committee told the public it would do: 16 of 18 September dots, "almost all participants" in the October minutes, and a market path for December that participants explicitly said matched their own view and used to justify waiting. Every data release since has been on the hawkish side: core CPI +0.3 again, PCE inflation rising to about 3.8 percent headline and 3.2-3.3 percent core, payrolls +165,000 with upward revisions, GDP +3.4 percent. Waller would hike 25 even on better data; Barr expects to raise; Cook and Hammack already voted for it; Jefferson keys on inflation; "a few" October holders saw it as a close call. Waller's own logic is that failing to deliver priced hikes would itself ease policy. Holding again at the last meeting of the year would contradict the SEP and the "will deliver price stability" language. 25 bp is also the size consistent with the "gradual approach" some favor and with fragile AI credit markets.

Hike of 50 bp or more
- Waller set a public, specific condition for considering 50 (core CPI around 0.3 or higher) and the data met it; he said 25 alone would put credibility at risk. The minutes record "a couple" open to more than 25 if inflation data were unfavorable, and "a few" warning that delay risks a larger, more disruptive tightening later. Cook stresses urgency and getting behind the curve. Having skipped October, a 50 bp move would simply put the Committee where two consecutive 25s would have. Four September dots were already at 4.375 for year-end, which requires 50 bp from here. Warsh rejects forward guidance and is willing to surprise. A hot October PCE (Nov 25) or strong November jobs report with firm wages (Dec 4) would strengthen this camp.

## 4. What I am least sure of, and what it does to the probabilities

- Warsh himself. He submits no dot, refuses guidance, and has run the Committee for only five months. I cannot tell from the pack whether his October patience reflects a durable preference for letting financial conditions do the work (favors hold) or a one-off before an "information-rich" interval that has now delivered hawkish information (favors hike). This is the main reason I keep hold in the mid-teens rather than single digits.
- Financial markets between Nov 23 and Dec 9. The pack contains no market data after early-to-mid November - only officials' references to a tech and AI-credit selloff of unknown depth. A sharp further leg down is the most plausible route to a hold and the only route to a cut. This widens the left side of the distribution.
- How much weight Waller's 50 bp trial balloon carries. Inference: it is one governor plus "a couple" in the minutes, against a center that preferred to hold just six weeks earlier, at a time when financial conditions are tightening on their own and oil has rolled over. Core CPI matched rather than exceeded his threshold. I treat 50 as a real but minority outcome, with a few points of extra weight in case the Nov 25 PCE and Dec 4 jobs data are both hot.
- Data still to come. October PCE is largely known from CPI and PPI, so the surprise potential is modest; the November jobs report is the larger swing factor. A weak report raises hold; a strong one raises 50. These roughly offset and mostly take probability from the 25 bp center rather than shift it.
- Geopolitics. A US-Iran agreement would lower headline inflation prospects and give the patient camp an argument; renewed escalation would do the opposite for inflation but raise uncertainty. Net effect: slightly more weight on hold.

Net: the uncertainties are two-sided around a well-signposted 25 bp hike, with the hold tail somewhat fatter than the 50 tail because the Committee has already shown it will wait and because financial-stability worries are rising.
