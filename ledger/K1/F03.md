# F03 forecast: FOMC decision of 8-9 December 2026 (change in upper bound of the target range)

Source: the evidence pack cp/K1/pack.md only (22 official documents, captured 2026-11-26). The upper bound in effect before the meeting is 4.00 percent (target range 3-3/4 to 4 percent).

## 1. What the Committee has done and what it signals

**Recent actions**
- September 15-16: raised the range by 25 bp to 3-3/4 to 4 percent ("removed a dose of accommodation").
- October 27-28: held, by a 10-2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike.

**September SEP (the only dot plot in the pack)**
- End-2026 dots: 12 of 18 at 4.125, 4 at 4.375, 2 at 3.875.
- So 16 of 18 participants wrote down at least one more hike in 2026, and December is the only meeting left.

**October minutes (released November 18)**
- "Almost all participants" judged that further action to raise the range, "broadly consistent with the September SEP", would likely be appropriate.
- "Some" participants said market pricing of tightening "in December and next year" matched their own view of the appropriate path, and several saw this as what made holding in October safe.
- A "few" who supported the hold called it a close call; "some" (more than the two dissenting voters) favoured hiking in October.
- "A couple" said an increase of more than 25 bp could warrant consideration at the next meeting if inflation data were unfavourable.
- Futures assigned "high probabilities" to a 25 bp hike by year-end; the Desk survey median expected a hike in December and another in March 2027.
- Counterweights: a majority noted tighter financial conditions since September, and "a couple" favoured a gradual approach to firming.

**Members since the meeting**
- Waller (November 10, before the CPI): would support 25 bp in December even if October inflation improved. If core CPI came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher", 50 bp "should be considered", and "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk". Core CPI then printed 0.3.
- Cook (November 20): "waiting to act ... has a cost"; sceptical that one hike is enough; sees no evidence inflation is returning to a sustainable path.
- Barr (November 18): "I expect it will be appropriate to raise rates further."
- Jefferson (November 13): inflation readings show "persistent price pressures"; the trajectory of inflation is "the more important signal". Leans hike without committing.
- Bowman (November 10): the most cautious. "Still work to do" on inflation, but the benefit of more tightening "has to be carefully weighed against its costs for interest-rate-sensitive sectors", and financial conditions have tightened.
- Warsh: refuses forward guidance and will not prejudge. He stresses "we don't just talk, we act", "we will deliver price stability", and that the hold was to gather information in an "information-rich" interval. He also says the economy is not judged "too hot" and that tighter financial conditions show policy is working.

**Reading:** the Committee has all but pre-announced a December hike through the SEP, the minutes and several speeches. The October hold was justified by the very expectation that the hike would come later. Waller's own logic is that promised hikes must be carried out or policy loosens. The base case is +25 bp.

## 2. Latest official data

**Inflation (pushes toward a hike, and toward a larger one at the margin)**
- October CPI: headline +0.4 percent; core +0.3 percent for the third straight month. Increases were broad: recreation, medical care, motor insurance, food away from home, transportation services +0.8.
- October PCE prices: +0.3 percent headline and core; 12-month rates 3.8 percent headline and 3.3 percent core, up from 3.7 and 3.1 in September.
- Core PCE at 3.3 percent is already at the low edge of the September SEP central tendency for Q4 (3.3 to 3.4).
- Beige Book: prices up moderately, and contacts expect further increases.
- Mild offsets: energy CPI slowed to +0.7 percent after +5.2; Bowman reports oil fell sharply in early November on hopes of a US-Iran agreement; used car prices fell 1.2 percent.

**Employment (removes any labour-market reason to wait)**
- October payrolls +165,000, with +84,000 of revisions to August and September.
- Unemployment 4.1 percent; average hourly earnings +0.4 percent on the month and 3.1 percent over the year.
- Initial claims near historical lows (four-week average about 195,000, per Cook).
- Soft spots: part-time for economic reasons +339,000; long-term unemployed 27.6 percent of the unemployed; a "low hire, low fire" market.

**Activity (pushes toward a hike, with a softer early-Q4 tone)**
- Q3 real GDP revised up to 4.3 percent from 3.4; real final sales to private domestic purchasers 5.8 percent.
- October real PCE was flat, and the saving rate was 4.1 percent.
- Beige Book: only slight to moderate growth in eight districts and no change in four; housing, construction and autos weak.
- Financial conditions have tightened: wider spreads, lower AI-related equity valuations, higher mortgage rates.

**Net:** inflation is high and broadening, the labour market is firm, and growth is strong. The dovish offsets are tighter financial conditions, the AI repricing, weak rate-sensitive sectors, flat October real spending and lower oil prices.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

**Cut of 50 bp or more.** Only a disorderly financial crisis before December 9 would do it. The candidates in the pack are the AI-debt and hedge-fund-leverage tail scenarios in the financial stability discussion, the French sovereign stress, and the Binance cyber heist. Even then the Fed would more likely use liquidity tools and hold the rate with core PCE at 3.3 percent.

**Cut of 25 bp.** The same crisis logic in milder form, or an abrupt political capitulation; the President's public criticism of the Board is in the pack. No participant in the pack advocates easing and the staff sees inflation risks skewed up. Close to negligible.

**Hold.** The Committee held in October even though "most" were on board with a later hike, and the Chairman refuses to prejudge.
- Financial conditions are doing some of the work, and Warsh treats that as the "dominant means" of policy.
- An AI-equity or credit sell-off could deepen in the two weeks before the meeting.
- Oil fell on hopes of an Iran deal, October real PCE was flat, and Bowman explicitly weighs the costs of further tightening.
- A weak November jobs report on December 4 could add to the case.
- "A gradual approach" and "not about some fixed individual date on a calendar" both give cover for a second pause.

**Hike of 25 bp.** This is the path the Committee described for itself.
- 16 of 18 September dots, and "almost all" participants in the October minutes, pointed to further tightening.
- Market pricing of a December hike was cited by participants as matching their own views.
- Waller, Barr, Cook and Hammack are explicit; Jefferson leans the same way.
- Every major release since October came in firm or hot.
- Not delivering would loosen conditions and damage the credibility the Chairman has staked on "we will deliver price stability". A quarter point also fits the gradualism the majority showed in October.

**Hike of 50 bp or more.** Waller set a public tripwire, core CPI around 0.3 percent or higher, and it was met.
- Core PCE accelerated to 3.3 percent year on year, GDP was revised to 4.3 percent, and payrolls were strong.
- "A couple" of participants had already floated more than 25 bp for December.
- Having skipped October, a 50 bp move could be framed as catching up to the September SEP path, on which four participants wanted two more hikes this year.
- A Chairman who disdains forward guidance and emphasises acting is less bound by market pricing than his predecessors.

## 4. What I am least sure of

- **The Chairman's reaction function.** Warsh gives no guidance, held in October against expectations of some colleagues, and stresses both resolve and the work being done by tighter financial conditions. This is the main reason hold is not in the low single digits. It also keeps some weight on 50 bp.
- **Financial-market developments between November 26 and December 9.** The pack describes an AI-related repricing under way in mid-November, wider spreads and French sovereign stress. A sharp deterioration would move weight from the hikes to hold, and a little to cuts. The pack has no market data after mid-November, so this widens the distribution.
- **How much support 50 bp has.** Only Waller is explicit and only "a couple" raised it in October. The speeches by Cook, Barr and Jefferson after the CPI did not call for 50 bp. A Committee that held 10-2 six weeks earlier, with a majority noting tighter financial conditions, is unlikely to jump to a half point. The data met Waller's condition, though, so I keep the probability near one in ten.
- **Data still to come.** The November employment report is due December 4. November CPI is due December 10, after the decision. A very weak jobs number would raise hold modestly; a strong one would firm up 25 bp and add a little to 50 bp.
- **Committee composition.** A White House proceeding on Governor Cook's future is pending. It could change a vote count but probably not the outcome.

Overall, the uncertainty is about size and about a possible second pause, not about direction. It widens the tails around +25 bp but does not displace it as the clear mode.
