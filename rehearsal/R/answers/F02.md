# F02 - FOMC 8-9 December 2026: change in the upper bound of the target range

Source: evidence pack R only (21 official documents, cutoff 2026-11-23T07:06Z). Upper bound in effect before the meeting: 4.00 percent (range 3-3/4 to 4).

## 1. What the Committee did and what it signals

Known facts from the pack:

- September 15-16: raised the range 25 bp to 3-3/4 to 4 percent ("removed a dose of accommodation").
- October 27-28: held, 10-2. Cook and Hammack dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike. The statement said more information "will help the Committee determine the policy stance" and "The Committee will deliver price stability."
- October minutes: "almost all participants" judged that further increases, "broadly consistent with the September SEP", would likely be appropriate. "Some" wanted to hike in October already; "a few" of the hold voters called it a close call; "a couple" said more than 25 bp could warrant consideration in December if inflation data were unfavourable; "a couple" favoured a gradual approach to firming.
- September SEP dots for end-2026 (midpoint): 2 at 3.875 (no further hike), 12 at 4.125 (one more), 4 at 4.375 (two more). December is the only meeting left, so 16 of 18 participants had at least one more hike pencilled in.
- Market pricing as reported in the minutes: futures put a high probability on a 25 bp hike by year-end; the Desk survey median had one hike in December.
- Waller (10 Nov, written before the CPI release): would support 25 bp in December even if inflation improved; if October core CPI came in "around 0.3 percent or even higher", 50 bp "should be considered" and "hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk". He also argued that promised hikes must be delivered in a reasonable time or financial conditions loosen.
- Bowman (10 Nov, after CPI): "still work to do", but "the benefit of additional policy tightening has to be carefully weighed against its costs", and she wants clarity on recent market volatility. Not committed.
- Jefferson (13 Nov): inflation trajectory is "the more important signal"; no commitment on timing or size.
- Barr (18 Nov): expects "it will be appropriate to raise rates further".
- Cook (20 Nov): waiting has a cost; one 25 bp hike is unlikely to be enough; "number and magnitude" of future moves depend on the data.
- Warsh (28 Oct press conference): refuses forward guidance and will not prejudge December, but says "we don't just talk - we act", welcomes tighter financial conditions as policy working, and describes the coming period as information rich.

Inference: the Committee's centre of gravity is a 25 bp hike in December. The October skip was framed as timing, not as a change of direction.

## 2. Latest official data and which way each pushes

Inflation - pushes toward a hike, and somewhat toward a larger one:
- October CPI: headline +0.4 percent m/m (after +0.6), core +0.3 percent for the third straight month. Waller's stated trigger for considering 50 bp (core around 0.3 or higher) was met. Broad gains in recreation, medical care, motor vehicle insurance, transportation services (+0.8), food away from home (+0.5).
- September PCE: headline +0.5 percent m/m, 3.6 percent y/y; core +0.3 percent m/m, 3.1 percent y/y.
- Officials' estimates for October PCE: headline about 3.8 percent, core 3.2 to 3.3 percent (Cook, Bowman). No disinflation in train.
- Offsetting detail: headline CPI decelerated, gasoline rose only 1.0 percent, and Bowman reports Brent fell sharply in early November on hopes of a US-Iran agreement.

Employment - no obstacle to a hike:
- October payrolls +165,000, prior two months revised up 84,000; unemployment 4.1 percent; average hourly earnings +0.4 percent m/m, 3.1 percent y/y.
- Soft spots: involuntary part-time up 339,000; long-term unemployed 27.6 percent of the unemployed; low hiring. These do not show a labour market that needs protecting from a 25 bp move.

Activity - supports a hike:
- Q3 real GDP +3.4 percent annualised; real final sales to private domestic purchasers +4.9 percent.
- Counterweights: real PCE flat in September, real disposable income -0.3 percent, saving rate 3.8 percent; October Beige Book showed only slight-to-modest growth in most Districts; housing and other rate-sensitive sectors weak.

Financial conditions - the main argument for caution:
- Wider credit spreads, lower AI-related equity valuations, higher mortgage rates, a stronger dollar, French sovereign stress. Barr (16 Nov) refers to investor questions about the AI boom "these past couple of weeks"; the Financial Stability Report flags AI-related leverage. This weighs against 50 bp and is the only live route to a hold.

## 3. Strongest case for each outcome

- cut50 (cut of 50 bp or more): only a sudden financial crisis - a disorderly AI-debt unwind or market-functioning breakdown - severe enough to override 3.8 percent inflation. Nothing in the pack points there; staff judged the banking system resilient.
- cut25: the same shock in milder form, or an abrupt collapse in activity. No participant has mentioned easing; the risk is purely tail.
- hold: the Committee already skipped once for "more information". Financial conditions have tightened since September, AI equities have been repricing, oil has dropped on Iran-deal hopes, Bowman explicitly weighs the costs of more tightening, and Warsh rejects calendar-based commitments. If the market slide deepens before 9 December, or the November jobs report (4 Dec) is weak, another skip is conceivable.
- hike25: this is the path described by almost all participants, by 12 of 18 September dots, by the Desk survey and by futures. Waller, Barr, Cook and Hammack are openly for more tightening; the data since October (core CPI 0.3, payrolls +165,000, GDP 3.4 percent) removed the reasons for waiting; and Waller's point that undelivered hikes loosen conditions gives even cautious members a reason to follow through. A quarter point is consistent with the gradualism shown in October.
- hike50 (hike of 50 bp or more): Waller's own trigger was met by the CPI report; four September dots implied two more hikes this year; two members already dissented for tightening in October; a couple of participants raised a larger move in the minutes; and having skipped October, a half point would simply restore a 25-bp-per-meeting pace. A chairman who says "no ifs, ands, or buts" could choose a decisive catch-up.

## 4. What I am least sure of

- How far the November AI-equity and credit repricing has gone and whether it worsens before the meeting. The pack has speeches, not market data. This is the main reason hold is above a token probability.
- Warsh's own preference. He gives no guidance, submits no dot and already surprised some by holding in October. This widens both tails (hold and 50 bp).
- How many members share Waller's 50 bp view. Only "a couple" raised it in October, and no other speaker after the CPI release endorsed it. I read 50 bp as a real but minority option (inference).
- Unseen data: October PCE (25 Nov) and November payrolls (4 Dec). November CPI lands after the meeting, so there is little scope for inflation data to argue against a hike.
- Geopolitics: a US-Iran agreement would lower energy prices but would not move core inflation quickly; an escalation would raise both inflation and uncertainty.

Net effect: these uncertainties keep hike25 well short of certainty and split the remainder roughly evenly between hold and hike50, with cuts as a remote tail.
